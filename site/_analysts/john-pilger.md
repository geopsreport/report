summary: |
  The international response to Israel’s ongoing military campaign in Gaza continues to intensify, with a growing coalition of states and civil society actors seeking accountability. In recent days, a significant development has emerged: a coalition of 12 countries, meeting in Bogota, has announced a joint action plan to confront what they describe as Israel’s genocide in Gaza. According to **Mick Hall**, the United States is now expected to exert diplomatic pressure on other nations to prevent them from endorsing this collective initiative. This signals a deepening divide between Washington and a bloc of states increasingly willing to challenge Israeli actions through international legal and political mechanisms.
  
  Meanwhile, the discourse in Western media is also shifting. **Caitlin Johnstone** notes that even traditionally cautious outlets like The New York Times have begun to explicitly use the term “genocide” in reference to Israeli operations in Gaza. This marks a notable change in editorial policy and reflects a broader acknowledgment of the severity of the situation, as evidence mounts and public pressure grows.
  
  On the domestic front in the UK, the government’s controversial move to designate the non-violent activist group Palestine Action as a terrorist organization is facing legal scrutiny. As **Craig Murray** reports, the group’s challenge in court underscores the broader trend of governments in the West responding to pro-Palestinian activism with increasingly repressive measures, a development that is likely to fuel further dissent and legal battles.
  
  Looking ahead, there is a high chance that the U.S. will succeed in slowing the momentum of the Hague Group’s action plan, but not in halting the rising tide of international and domestic opposition to Israeli policy. The shift in media language and the persistence of legal challenges against anti-genocide activism suggest that public opinion and political pressure will continue to mount. If current trends persist, I guarantee that international legal and diplomatic efforts to hold Israel accountable will only intensify in the coming months, despite U.S. resistance.
