summary: |
  As of mid-July 2025, the international response to Israel’s actions in Gaza has entered a new phase, with a coalition of 12 countries—dubbed the Hague Group—announcing a joint action plan to confront what they term Israel’s “genocide.” This marks a significant escalation in diplomatic efforts, as previous months saw mounting evidence and public discourse shifting toward explicit recognition of the severity of the crisis.
  
  The most recent development, according to **Mick Hall**, is the expectation that the United States will actively pressure other nations not to endorse the Hague Group’s plan, signaling Washington’s continued support for Israel despite growing global condemnation. This U.S. stance is likely to create further diplomatic friction, especially among Global South countries and European states facing domestic pressure to act.
  
  Meanwhile, media narratives are also shifting. **Caitlin Johnstone** notes that even traditionally cautious outlets like The New York Times are now openly using the term “genocide” in reference to Gaza, reflecting a broader change in mainstream Western discourse. This rhetorical shift could influence public opinion and policy debates in the U.S. and Europe, making it harder for governments to maintain the status quo.
  
  On the legal front, the UK government’s attempt to designate the non-violent group Palestine Action as a terrorist organization is being challenged in court, as reported by **Craig Murray**. The outcome of this case could set a precedent for how anti-genocide activism is treated in Western democracies and may affect the broader protest movement.
  
  **Trends and Predictions:**  
  The international coalition against Israel’s Gaza policy is likely to grow, though U.S. opposition will slow the formation of a unified global front. The mainstreaming of the “genocide” label in Western media is expected to intensify political pressure on allied governments. Legal challenges to anti-genocide activism in the UK and elsewhere will continue, but there is a high chance that such designations will be overturned or softened in court, given the non-violent nature of the groups involved. Most definitely, the coming weeks will see increased polarization between U.S.-led allies and the Hague Group, with diplomatic, legal, and media battles intensifying.
