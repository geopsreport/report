summary: |
  The international response to Israel’s actions in Gaza continues to intensify, with a significant development emerging from the recent joint action plan announced by 12 countries in Bogota. As **Mick Hall** reports, the United States is now actively pressuring other nations not to endorse these collective measures aimed at confronting what is increasingly being described as genocide in Gaza. This marks a notable escalation in diplomatic maneuvering, as Washington seeks to limit the scope and impact of multilateral action against Israel.
  
  Meanwhile, the shifting discourse in Western media has reached a new milestone. **Caitlin Johnstone** observes that even traditionally cautious outlets like The New York Times have ceased avoiding the term “genocide” in their coverage, reflecting a broader acceptance of the severity of the situation. This shift is likely to influence public opinion and, potentially, policy debates in the U.S. and Europe.
  
  Domestically, the UK government’s attempt to designate the non-violent group Palestine Action as a terrorist organization is facing legal challenges. **Craig Murray** details the group’s request for temporary relief from this designation, highlighting the growing tension between state security measures and civil liberties in the context of pro-Palestinian activism.
  
  Looking ahead, the trends point to a deepening divide between Western governments and segments of their own societies, as well as with the broader international community. There is a high chance that U.S. efforts to stifle collective action will face resistance, particularly as media narratives evolve and legal challenges mount. I guarantee that the debate over the definition and prosecution of genocide in Gaza will remain at the forefront of international relations in the coming weeks, with further polarization likely both within and between states.
