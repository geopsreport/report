As the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran persists into its third week, the conflict’s military, technological, and political dimensions are evolving, with new developments underscoring the limits of Western power projection and the shifting dynamics of regional deterrence.

**Iran’s Missile Salvos and Air Defense Strain**

The intensity of Iran’s missile campaign is now a defining feature of the conflict. According to **Larry C. Johnson**, Iran’s “overwhelming missile capabilities” have inflicted significant damage on Israel, exposing the limitations of U.S.-supplied THAAD and Patriot PAC-3 air defense systems. Johnson notes that these systems are “kaput or soon will be,” as the sheer volume of Iranian projectiles saturates defensive networks, leading to a protracted and costly war of attrition. This assessment is echoed by other analysts, who point to the inability of superior Israeli air power to decisively counter Iran’s missile barrages.

**U.S. Military Technology: Railguns and Next-Gen Fighters**

Amid the ongoing air campaign, the U.S. is accelerating the deployment of advanced military technologies. **Caleb Larson** reports that the Navy has resumed testing electromagnetic railguns, with the latest NAVSEA findings suggesting these Mach 7 weapons could soon be mounted on Trump-Class battleships. In the air domain, **Kris Osborn** highlights the operational debut of the Boeing F-47 ‘NGAD’ sixth-generation fighter, equipped with Northrop Grumman’s Stand-in Attack Weapon (SiAW). Osborn explains that this combination “effectively targets and neutralizes advanced Russian air defense systems like the S-400,” reinforcing U.S. air superiority against Iranian and allied defenses.

**Strategic Ambiguity and the Erosion of U.S. Dominance**

The war’s expansion into shadowy, ambiguous operations is altering perceptions of American power in the region. **Elijah J. Magnier** observes that Iran’s sustained confrontations are “challenging the image of U.S. dominance,” as the battlefield extends beyond traditional fronts and includes unclaimed strikes in the Gulf. This strategic ambiguity, Magnier argues, is undermining Washington’s aura of invulnerability and emboldening regional actors to test U.S. red lines.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of military stalemate and strategic uncertainty. Iran’s missile saturation tactics are eroding the effectiveness of Western air defenses, while the U.S. responds with technological escalation. The conflict’s ambiguous expansion and the visible strain on U.S. and Israeli capabilities are shifting regional perceptions of power. Most analysts foresee continued attritional warfare, with the risk of further escalation—especially if new technologies are deployed in combat. The lack of a clear diplomatic off-ramp suggests the humanitarian and economic crises will deepen, and the long-term consequences for U.S. influence in the Middle East remain highly uncertain.
