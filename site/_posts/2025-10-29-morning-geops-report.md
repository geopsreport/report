As the Gaza ceasefire remains fragile and global power dynamics continue to shift, the past half-day has brought new insights into the interplay of military, economic, and technological forces shaping the geopolitical landscape.

**West Bank: Surge in Settler Violence and International Alarm**

While attention has focused on Gaza, violence in the occupied West Bank is escalating. The UN Human Rights Office, as reported by **Chris Hedges**, warns of a “significant increase in Israeli settler violence and outpost construction,” particularly during the olive harvest. A recent attack left a Palestinian woman hospitalized, underscoring the acute risks faced by local communities. Analysts note that this surge in violence, largely unchecked by Israeli authorities, is heightening tensions and fueling calls for greater international intervention.

**Digital Censorship and the Palestinian Struggle**

The intersection of technology and the Israel-Palestine conflict is drawing renewed scrutiny. In a recent interview, Professor Omar Zahzah told **Robert Scheer** that “corporate interests and digital censorship are shaping the narrative of the Palestinian liberation struggle,” as detailed in Zahzah’s book, *Terms of Servitude*. The conversation highlights how major tech platforms, under pressure from both governments and private actors, are restricting pro-Palestinian content and activism online—raising concerns about freedom of expression and the ability of grassroots movements to mobilize.

**Africa: IMF Policies and Sovereignty Concerns**

Beyond the Middle East, economic sovereignty in Africa is under threat. According to a report cited by **Vijay Prashad**, Senegal’s Court of Auditors has found that the IMF’s endorsement of irregular financial practices has “compromised national sovereignty and benefited multinational corporations.” This revelation adds to growing criticism of Western-led financial institutions and their impact on African governance, with analysts warning that such practices risk entrenching economic dependency and undermining local accountability.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of intensifying contestation—whether through settler violence in the West Bank, digital censorship of dissent, or economic interventions in Africa. The majority of analysts agree that these developments are deepening mistrust in international institutions and fueling demands for accountability. Looking ahead, expect continued grassroots resistance and international advocacy, but also persistent efforts by state and corporate actors to maintain control. Without substantive policy shifts, the prospects for justice and stability in these regions remain limited.
