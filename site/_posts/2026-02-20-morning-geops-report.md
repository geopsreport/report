As the global order remains unsettled by overlapping crises, the last 12 hours have brought new insights into the U.S.-Iran confrontation, the unraveling of arms control, and the growing risk of financial instability.

**U.S.-Iran: Misinformation and Congressional Scrutiny**

Tensions between the U.S. and Iran continue to escalate amid the largest American military buildup in the Gulf since 2003. New analysis from **Larry C. Johnson** highlights a wave of questionable claims regarding Iran’s missile capabilities and Israel’s defensive effectiveness, as reported in Sy Hersh’s recent article on the Trump-Netanyahu meeting. Johnson points out that “the narrative being pushed about Iran’s threat is riddled with exaggerations and strategic disinformation,” raising concerns about the reliability of the intelligence shaping policy decisions.

Meanwhile, domestic calls for accountability are intensifying. **Ray McGovern** reports that Representative Ro Khanna is urging lawmakers to publicly declare their positions on potential military action against Iran, even if a vote occurs after hostilities have begun. This reflects growing unease in Congress about the lack of transparency and the risk of being sidelined in decisions that could lead to war.

**End of Arms Control: The New Nuclear Race**

The expiration of the New START Treaty this month marks a pivotal moment in global security. **Daniel L. Davis** warns that “the end of the global strategic arms control system” has arrived, with the U.S. now moving toward a trilateral nuclear strategy that factors in China’s rapidly expanding arsenal. Analysts broadly agree that this shift is likely to accelerate a new nuclear arms race, with few diplomatic guardrails left to prevent escalation.

**Financial Instability: Market Bubbles and Crash Fears**

Economic risks are also mounting. **Jack Rasmus** notes that financial asset market bubbles, inflated since Trump’s return to office, have suffered significant contractions in early February. He cautions that “the sustainability of these bubbles is in serious doubt,” and the potential for a financial crash is rising as global instability feeds market anxiety.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trend is one of mounting instability and diminishing checks on escalation—military, political, and economic. The U.S.-Iran standoff is increasingly shaped by contested narratives and domestic political maneuvering, while the collapse of arms control frameworks opens the door to a new era of nuclear competition. Simultaneously, the risk of a financial crisis is growing, adding another layer of uncertainty to an already volatile global landscape.

Looking ahead, analysts expect continued brinkmanship in the Gulf, a rapid deterioration in nuclear stability, and heightened vigilance over financial markets. The coming days will test the resilience of political institutions and the capacity of global leaders to manage converging crises.
