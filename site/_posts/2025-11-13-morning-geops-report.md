As the international order remains unsettled, the past 12 hours have brought new insights into U.S. foreign policy controversies, Russia’s internal and external challenges, and the evolving debate over the nature of American power. Independent analysts point to mounting volatility and a deepening crisis of legitimacy for established powers.

**Venezuela: Fresh Evidence of U.S. Complicity in 2020 Coup Attempt**

Newly released federal court documents have intensified scrutiny of the Trump administration’s role in the failed 2020 coup in Venezuela. **Max Blumenthal** reports that these files “implicate Trump associates and suggest prior knowledge of the plot by White House insiders,” raising questions about the legality and strategic wisdom of U.S. regime-change efforts. The revelations come as Washington maintains sanctions and military pressure on Caracas, including recent B-52 bomber flights near Venezuelan airspace. Analysts warn that these disclosures will further erode U.S. credibility in Latin America and may embolden regional actors to resist American influence.

**Russia-Ukraine: Manpower Shortages and Political Fallout**

On the Russia-Ukraine front, the slow progress of Russian advances in 2023 and 2024 is now widely attributed to manpower shortages. **Robert Barnes**, as cited by **Larry C. Johnson**, argues that “Russian troop shortages have fundamentally shaped the conflict’s trajectory,” rather than tactical or leadership failures. This assessment is echoed by a growing consensus that internal divisions within Russia—between the Foreign Ministry’s integrationist approach and the FSB’s hardline stance—are complicating both military and diplomatic strategies. Meanwhile, U.S. officials continue to tout the impact of sanctions, with former Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent** claiming they are causing “immediate pain” to Russia’s economy, though Russian sources dispute the severity.

**The U.S. Global Image: Empire, Intervention, and Domestic Dissent**

A broader debate over the character of American power is gaining momentum. **Caitlin Johnstone** contrasts the British Empire’s open embrace of imperialism with the U.S. tendency to justify interventions as battles against dictators or terrorists, masking underlying imperial motives. This critique is sharpened by the latest Venezuela disclosures and continued U.S. support for controversial allies. Domestically, these issues, combined with persistent economic and political instability, are fueling populist skepticism toward both major parties and their foreign policy agendas—a trend highlighted by **Alastair Crooke**, who notes that “populist discontent” is now a structural feature of American politics.

**Trends and Outlook**

The majority of independent analysts agree that the exposure of covert U.S. actions in Venezuela will further strain Washington’s standing in Latin America and could trigger greater resistance to its policies. In Russia, manpower constraints and war fatigue are expected to limit the scope of further advances, increasing pressure for a negotiated settlement, though no breakthrough is anticipated. The U.S. faces mounting challenges to its global image, with critics warning that continued interventionism and domestic unrest may undermine its ability to project power and maintain alliances.

Looking ahead, unresolved conflicts, new disclosures, and shifting public sentiment point to a period of heightened volatility and uncertainty in global affairs. Most analysts expect these trends to intensify, with established powers facing growing resistance both abroad and at home.
