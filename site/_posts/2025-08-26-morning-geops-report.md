As the crises in Gaza, Ukraine, and the broader Middle East continue to dominate the geopolitical agenda, the past 12 hours have brought new perspectives on the roots of violence in Palestine, the evolution of Russian military strategy, and the shifting energy landscape in the Gulf.

**Gaza: Empathy and the Roots of Resistance**

Amid ongoing famine and civilian suffering in Gaza, the debate over Palestinian resistance has sharpened. **Caitlin Johnstone** argues that critics who condemn Hamas while offering only vague support for Palestine “lack empathy and humility,” failing to grasp the desperation that drives extreme actions under occupation. Johnstone contends that “people will do desperate things when subjected to desperate conditions,” emphasizing the need to understand the context behind the October 7 attacks and the broader cycle of violence. This view is increasingly echoed among independent analysts, who stress that meaningful progress requires addressing the underlying causes of Palestinian despair rather than focusing solely on condemnation.

**Russia: Military Resilience and Strategic Adaptation**

On the Russia-Ukraine front, new insights have emerged regarding Moscow’s military posture and diplomatic approach. **Larry C. Johnson** highlights the role of General Evgeny Buzhinsky in shaping Russia’s military diplomacy and arms control efforts since the Soviet collapse. Johnson notes that Buzhinsky’s experience “demonstrates how the Russian military survived and adapted after the USSR’s dissolution,” maintaining strategic capabilities despite economic and political turmoil. This historical resilience, analysts suggest, underpins Russia’s current negotiating stance and its ability to withstand external pressure as the war in Ukraine drags on.

**Gulf Region: Saudi Arabia’s Energy Transition**

In the Gulf, Saudi Arabia’s push toward renewable energy has gained renewed attention. **Chris Hedges** reports that the kingdom’s embrace of solar and wind power reflects a paradox: while still a major oil producer, Saudi Arabia is seeking to diversify its economy and meet rising domestic electricity demand through renewables. Hedges observes that this shift is “driven by the logic of selling oil abroad to fund Vision 2030,” signaling a pragmatic adaptation to global energy trends. The move is seen as both a response to international climate pressures and a strategic effort to secure long-term economic stability.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to deepening humanitarian and political crises in Gaza, with calls for greater empathy and structural change gaining traction among independent voices. Russia’s military resilience continues to shape its approach to the Ukraine conflict, suggesting a prolonged standoff absent significant diplomatic breakthroughs. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia’s energy transition signals a broader regional adaptation to shifting economic and environmental realities. Looking ahead, analysts expect continued polarization over the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, a sustained impasse in Ukraine, and incremental but significant changes in the Gulf’s energy strategy. Grassroots advocacy and non-Western actors are likely to play an increasingly influential role in shaping outcomes across these flashpoints.
