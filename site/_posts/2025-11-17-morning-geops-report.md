As global power struggles intensify, the past 12 hours have seen new developments in Western domestic politics, the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and the ongoing debate over surveillance and civil liberties, each reflecting deeper structural tensions within and between states.

**U.S. Domestic Politics: Immigration, Surveillance, and Political Messaging**

Amid the ongoing government shutdown, controversy has erupted over the use of taxpayer funds for political advertising. **Chris Hedges** reports that South Dakota Governor Kristi Noem filmed a high-profile immigration ad at Mount Rushmore, funded through a $220 million Department of Homeland Security contract. Noem’s campaign, which frames a hardline immigration stance as essential, has drawn criticism for leveraging public resources during a period of acute federal dysfunction. This episode underscores the politicization of immigration and the growing use of state power for partisan messaging.

Simultaneously, the Supreme Court’s recent decision to allow Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to question and detain individuals based on race has sparked alarm among civil rights advocates. **Guadalupe Cardona** of Unión del Barrio, cited by Hedges, observes a marked increase in racial profiling and detentions of Latino individuals, describing the new norm as “a climate of fear and suspicion.” The expansion of ICE’s authority, coupled with the administration’s push for 24/7 social media monitoring, points to a significant tightening of domestic surveillance and enforcement.

**Israel-Palestine: Escalating Violence and International Scrutiny**

In the West Bank, violence has escalated further. **Chris Hedges** details the killing of two 15-year-old Palestinian boys by Israeli forces, with conflicting accounts from the Palestinian Authority and the Israeli military regarding the circumstances. Meanwhile, Israeli settlers reportedly torched a mosque, fueling concerns of a broader campaign of intimidation and collective punishment. These incidents have drawn condemnation from human rights organizations and heightened scrutiny of Israel’s conduct in the occupied territories, even as international diplomatic efforts remain stalled.

**Civil Liberties: Academic Detention and the Security State**

The reach of security measures has extended beyond borders. **Chris Hedges** also reports that Richard Falk, a 95-year-old human rights scholar, was detained and interrogated for hours by Canadian authorities while en route to a conference on Canada’s complicity in Israeli actions in Gaza. The incident, which officials justified on “national security” grounds, has been widely criticized as an example of academic repression and the growing use of security pretexts to stifle dissent.

**Trends and Outlook**

The convergence of political messaging, expanded surveillance, and security-driven repression signals a period of heightened state control and shrinking civic space in the U.S. and allied countries. In the Middle East, the cycle of violence and impunity continues to undermine prospects for peace and deepen regional instability. Analysts expect these trends to persist, with domestic polarization and international tensions likely to intensify as governments prioritize security and political advantage over rights and accountability. The coming days may see further backlash from civil society, but the prevailing trajectory points toward greater contestation and uncertainty across these critical arenas.
