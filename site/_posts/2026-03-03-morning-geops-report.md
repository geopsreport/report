As the US-Israeli war on Iran enters its second week, the conflict’s intensity and complexity continue to mount, with new developments highlighting both the operational challenges and the deepening humanitarian crisis. The assassination of Ayatollah Khamenei and the launch of Operation Epic Fury have failed to achieve a quick strategic victory, instead triggering fierce Iranian resistance and widespread civilian suffering.

**Iranian Military Resilience and US Setbacks**

Contrary to the expectations of US planners, Iranian forces have demonstrated notable resilience. **Larry C. Johnson** reports that “the Iranian military has successfully expelled US forces from multiple bases in the region amidst ongoing conflict,” directly challenging the narrative of US dominance and complicating coalition operations. This development underscores the distributed nature of Iranian power, which has allowed the state to maintain functionality and resistance even after the decapitation of its leadership.

**Escalating Civilian Toll and Humanitarian Crisis**

The humanitarian situation in Iran is rapidly deteriorating. **Caitlin Johnstone** relays harrowing witness accounts from Tehran, where a “devastating double-tap airstrike by the US-Israel alliance” has left scenes of chaos and severe civilian injuries. The psychological and physical toll on the population is compounded by reports, highlighted by **Dennis Kucinich** (via **John Pilger**), of “Iranian parents sifting through rubble in search of their missing daughters,” a stark illustration of the war’s impact on ordinary families. These accounts are fueling international outrage and raising urgent questions about the conduct of the war and adherence to international law.

**Air Power Dynamics and Coalition Strain**

On the military front, the US and Israeli air campaign continues to rely heavily on advanced platforms. **Daniel L. Davis** notes the “successful collaboration of F-22 Raptors and F-35 Lightning IIs in Operation Epic Fury,” which have transitioned from simulated adversaries to effective partners, demonstrating the effectiveness of fifth-generation air power. However, the operational tempo remains punishing. The US Navy, having depleted significant stocks of Tomahawk missiles during the initial “Shock and Anger” phase, now faces a potential shortage if a second front emerges in the Pacific, raising concerns about strategic overstretch.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing assessment among independent analysts is that the war is settling into a protracted and bloody phase, with neither side able to secure a decisive advantage. Iranian resistance is hardening, while the US and its allies face mounting operational, political, and humanitarian challenges. The legitimacy crisis surrounding the offensive is deepening, with calls for accountability and a diplomatic resolution growing louder but so far unanswered.

Looking ahead, the risk of regional spillover—especially into Lebanon—remains acute. The humanitarian crisis is expected to worsen, and the strain on coalition resources could have global implications, particularly if tensions with China escalate. The majority view is that without a dramatic shift toward diplomacy, the conflict will continue to destabilize the region and erode US influence, with unpredictable consequences for global security.
