As the regional crisis surrounding the US-Iran conflict enters its third month, the past 12 hours have brought fresh diplomatic maneuvers, new military posturing, and mounting economic anxieties, further entrenching the sense of uncertainty across the Middle East and beyond.

**Gulf States Seek Direct Dialogue with Iran**

A notable new development is the emergence of direct diplomatic overtures from Gulf Arab states toward Tehran. **Marwan Bishara** reports that Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have initiated discreet talks with Iranian officials, aiming to “establish deconfliction mechanisms and safeguard energy infrastructure.” This marks a significant shift from earlier reliance on US-led mediation, reflecting growing unease among Gulf leaders over the risks of regional escalation and the limitations of Western security guarantees. Bishara notes that “regional actors are increasingly taking matters into their own hands, wary of being caught in the crossfire of great power rivalry.”

**Israeli Cabinet Divisions Surface Over Lebanon Policy**

Within Israel, internal divisions are coming to the fore regarding the government’s approach to Lebanon. **Rania Khalek** highlights that several senior Israeli ministers are now publicly questioning the sustainability of continued military operations against Hezbollah, citing the “rising costs, limited strategic gains, and growing international isolation.” Cabinet debates reportedly center on whether to pursue a more limited engagement or to seek a negotiated security arrangement, with no consensus yet emerging. This internal discord is seen as further complicating Israel’s ability to project strength and maintain unity in the face of persistent cross-border tensions.

**Shipping Disruptions Spread to Suez Canal**

The economic fallout from the conflict is deepening, with new reports of shipping disruptions extending beyond the Red Sea into the Suez Canal. **Michael Hudson** notes that “insurance premiums for vessels transiting the Suez have surged, and several major shipping lines are now rerouting traffic around Africa, adding weeks to delivery times and driving up costs.” This development underscores the widening impact of regional instability on global trade, with supply chains facing compounding delays and price pressures.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of regional actors asserting greater agency as confidence in external mediation wanes. The Gulf states’ direct engagement with Iran and Israel’s internal policy debates both signal a shift toward pragmatic recalibration, though the risk of miscalculation remains high. Economically, the spread of shipping disruptions to the Suez Canal highlights the conflict’s growing global ramifications.

Looking ahead, analysts broadly expect continued diplomatic experimentation and operational adaptation, but warn that absent a breakthrough in negotiations, the region is likely to remain mired in volatility. The consensus is that only a sustained, inclusive diplomatic process—driven by regional stakeholders—offers a plausible path toward de-escalation and stability.
