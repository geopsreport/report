As global tensions persist into late December, new developments are shaping the geopolitical landscape, particularly in U.S.-Venezuela relations, the Ukraine conflict, and the evolving military-technological race between the U.S. and China. Recent reports highlight intensifying U.S. interventionism, deepening policy divides in Washington, and rapid advances in unmanned warfare.

**Venezuela: Congressional Dissent and Blockade Escalation**

The U.S. campaign against Venezuela has entered a more contentious phase. Four centrist Republican House members have broken ranks, joining Democrats in a discharge petition to extend Affordable Care Act subsidies, signaling broader fractures within the GOP as the party faces internal dissent over domestic and foreign policy. Meanwhile, House Republicans have rejected two War Powers resolutions intended to limit President Trump’s authority for airstrikes and potential military action against Venezuela. **Chris Hedges** describes this as “absolute dereliction of duty,” underscoring the erosion of Congressional checks on executive war powers. **Caitlin Johnstone** notes that Trump’s justification for the naval blockade—accusing Venezuela of “stealing” U.S. oil—is widely dismissed as a pretext for resource-driven interventionism, with analysts warning this could set the stage for direct conflict.

**Ukraine: U.S. Misreads Russian Intentions as EU Policy Hardens**

On the Ukraine front, the diplomatic impasse is compounded by misperceptions in Washington. **Larry C. Johnson** highlights reporting by Sy Hersh that Trump administration advisors, including Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, believe President Putin is seeking an exit due to internal pressures. However, Johnson argues this view is misguided, as Moscow remains committed to its core demands for security guarantees and a legally binding settlement—positions the U.S. continues to sidestep. Meanwhile, the EU’s indefinite freeze on Russian assets, led by Ursula von der Leyen, remains a flashpoint, with no new consensus emerging among member states.

**China: Drone ‘Mothership’ Ushers in New Era of Unmanned Warfare**

In the Indo-Pacific, China’s military modernization has taken a significant leap with the successful first flight of the Jiutian “drone carrier.” **Daniel L. Davis** reports that this “flying aircraft carrier” can deploy up to 100 drones at long range, dramatically enhancing Beijing’s capabilities in unmanned and swarm warfare. This development is seen as a strategic response to U.S. naval power and a harbinger of future conflict scenarios where drone swarms could challenge traditional force structures.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of institutional fragmentation and technological acceleration. In the U.S., Congressional divisions are weakening oversight of military interventions, while in Ukraine, Western policymakers risk strategic miscalculation by underestimating Russian resolve. In Asia, China’s rapid advances in unmanned systems are reshaping the regional balance, prompting concerns over an emerging arms race in drone and anti-access technologies.

Looking ahead, analysts expect further volatility as U.S. domestic divides complicate foreign policy, the Ukraine conflict remains deadlocked, and China’s military innovations force a reassessment of U.S. and allied defense strategies. The risk of escalation—whether in Latin America, Eastern Europe, or the Western Pacific—remains high absent a shift toward de-escalation and renewed diplomatic engagement.
