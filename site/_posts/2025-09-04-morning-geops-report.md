As global power centers grapple with overlapping crises, the last 12 hours have seen new developments in European military policy, the Ukraine conflict, and the ongoing debate over Western arms deals, with analysts highlighting the interplay between domestic instability and shifting international alignments.

**Europe’s Military Ambitions Undermined by Internal Crisis**

The Paris summit on forming a European "reassurance force" for Ukraine has been overshadowed by France’s deepening political turmoil and the limited capacity of Britain and other European states to contribute meaningfully. **Anatol Lieven** notes that “the French government is in tatters,” casting doubt on the viability of any substantial deployment and exposing the fragility of Europe’s collective security ambitions. The lack of clear direction from the summit reflects a broader trend: as internal political challenges mount, Europe’s ability to shape outcomes in Ukraine and project military power is increasingly constrained.

**Ukraine: Russian Confidence in Military Solution Grows**

Amid the stalled European response, Russian officials have signaled growing confidence in achieving a military resolution to the Ukraine conflict. **Gilbert Doctorow** reports that the Russian elite now widely believe “the conflict will ultimately be resolved through military means rather than diplomatic negotiations.” This assessment is reinforced by President Putin’s recent statements, which highlighted the importance of understandings reached with President Trump in Alaska, while also acknowledging the peace efforts of China, India, and other strategic partners. The prevailing view among analysts is that Moscow sees little incentive to compromise as battlefield dynamics shift in its favor.

**UK-Israel Arms Deal Sparks Controversy**

In the UK, the Labour government’s decision to award a £2 billion military training contract to Israel’s Elbit Systems has drawn sharp criticism from independent experts. **Andrew Feinstein**, **Paul Holden**, and **Jack Cinamon** question the appropriateness of selecting Israel’s largest arms firm—currently embroiled in a corruption scandal and closely linked to the Gaza conflict—to train British troops. The analysts argue that this move “raises serious ethical and strategic concerns,” especially given the intensifying scrutiny of Israel’s conduct in Gaza and the broader debate over Western complicity in regional conflicts.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to a weakening of Europe’s military and diplomatic leverage, with internal instability undermining collective action. In Ukraine, Russian confidence in a military solution is hardening, while Western divisions and war fatigue grow more pronounced. The UK’s controversial arms deal with Israel underscores the ethical dilemmas facing Western governments as they navigate alliances amid mounting humanitarian crises. Looking ahead, analysts expect further fragmentation of Western policy, increased Russian assertiveness, and heightened debate over the morality and effectiveness of military partnerships. The risk of escalation remains high, but the prospects for decisive breakthroughs appear increasingly remote.
