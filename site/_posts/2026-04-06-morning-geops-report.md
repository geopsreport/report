As the U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran grinds into its seventh week, the war’s ripple effects are intensifying across military, economic, and political fronts, with new developments underscoring the conflict’s global reach and growing unpredictability.

**China’s Expanding Role: Missile Aid and Intelligence Sharing**

A significant new dimension is China’s deepening involvement in support of Iran. According to **Daniel L. Davis**, “five ships have already delivered solid rocket fuel ingredients” to Iran, enabling Tehran to rebuild its missile program despite sustained U.S.-Israeli strikes. Chinese military intelligence is also leveraging AI and commercial satellites to track U.S. military activities, providing Iran with “detailed intelligence” that is complicating American operational planning. This marks a notable escalation in great-power competition, with Beijing’s support helping to blunt the effectiveness of Western sanctions and military pressure.

**U.S. Navy: Carrier Fleet Under Pressure, Gerald R. Ford’s Ordeal**

The operational strain on the U.S. Navy is becoming increasingly acute. **Davis** reports that the USS Gerald R. Ford, the largest and most advanced aircraft carrier ever built, has faced an “extended deployment, a shipboard fire, [and] 200 sailors treated for smoke,” forcing an unscheduled stop in Croatia for repairs. While the Ford remains a symbol of U.S. naval power, these setbacks highlight the challenges of sustaining high-tempo operations in a contested environment. The Navy is now accelerating the commissioning of new carriers to address gaps, but analysts warn that reliance on large, vulnerable platforms is being tested as never before.

**Political Fallout: Domestic Criticism and Calls for Restraint**

On the U.S. political front, criticism of the administration’s Iran policy is mounting. **Chris Hedges** highlights a statement by Marjorie Taylor Greene, who condemned President Trump’s “aggressive stance” and called for an end to hostilities, particularly on Easter, denouncing the administration’s claims of protecting the Iranian people as “hypocrisy.” This reflects growing divisions within the U.S. political establishment and a rising chorus of voices urging a shift toward diplomacy.

**Trends and Outlook**

The majority view among independent analysts is that the war is entering a new phase of attrition and global entanglement. China’s material and intelligence support is reinforcing Iran’s resilience, while U.S. military assets face mounting operational stress. Domestically, political pressure for de-escalation is intensifying, but no clear off-ramp has emerged. The risk of further escalation—including direct U.S.-China confrontation—remains elevated, and the conflict’s long-term impact on global power balances is becoming more pronounced. The coming days will likely see continued jockeying for leverage, with the possibility of diplomatic overtures tempered by the realities of deepening great-power rivalry.
