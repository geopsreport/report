As the US-Israeli campaign against Iran enters its third week, the conflict’s operational, economic, and diplomatic dimensions are evolving, with new developments highlighting both the resilience of Iranian defenses and the mounting global repercussions.

**Kharg Island: Disputed Claims and Iran’s Energy Leverage**

President Trump’s recent claims of successful strikes on Iran’s Kharg Island oil facilities have been met with skepticism. **Larry C. Johnson** dismisses these assertions as “unrealistic,” noting that “Iran’s warnings of retaliation against attacks on its energy infrastructure have not deterred a recent increase in oil exports from the island, indicating Iran’s preparedness for potential strikes.” This signals both Tehran’s determination to maintain energy leverage and the limits of US-Israeli targeting efforts. Iranian officials continue to threaten severe retaliation if Kharg’s infrastructure is hit, underscoring the risk of further escalation in the Gulf.

**Air War: Super Hornet’s Role and Stealth vs. S-400**

On the military front, the US Navy’s F/A-18E/F Super Hornet has emerged as a workhorse of Operation Epic Fury. **Steve Balestrieri** highlights the aircraft’s evolution from its initial role in Iraq to “a key component of the 2026 air campaign against Iran,” reflecting the adaptability of US air power amid high-tempo operations. Meanwhile, the technological contest between US stealth aircraft and Russian-made S-400 air defense systems remains in focus. **Harrison Kass** observes that, despite the S-400’s reputation as a “stealth killer,” “operational evidence indicates that stealth aircraft maintain the upper hand,” reinforcing the continued dominance of US fifth-generation platforms in contested airspace.

**Humanitarian and Economic Fallout: Global Energy and Social Tensions**

The ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz continues to disrupt global energy markets, with oil prices remaining above $120 per barrel. The ripple effects are particularly acute in South Asia; **Ray McGovern** notes that the LPG shortage in India is “resulting in significant fear and financial losses for individuals and businesses,” illustrating the broader humanitarian impact beyond the immediate war zone.

Meanwhile, the conflict’s social ramifications are becoming more pronounced. Rising antisemitism linked to Middle Eastern tensions has prompted public warnings. **Caitlin Johnstone** reports that Anti-Defamation League CEO Jonathan Greenblatt has condemned “the scapegoating of Jewish people for the actions of the Israeli government,” urging leaders to avoid fueling hateful conspiracy theories as violence and blame escalate globally.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of entrenched stalemate: Iran’s leadership remains resilient, US and Israeli operations face diminishing returns, and the humanitarian and economic crises are deepening. The technological edge of US air power persists, but the risk of escalation—especially over strategic energy infrastructure—remains high. Internationally, the conflict is fueling both economic instability and rising social tensions. Most analysts expect continued attrition, with no clear diplomatic breakthrough in sight and the potential for further regional destabilization if current patterns persist.
