As global instability persists, the past 12 hours have brought new revelations on U.S. inequality, the Ukraine conflict’s corruption scandal, and the evolving U.S. strategy toward Venezuela, highlighting the interconnected crises shaping the geopolitical landscape.

**U.S. Inequality: Alarming New Data Fuels Social Tensions**

A new report by **Lee Camp**, cited by **Ray McGovern**, reveals that wealth inequality in the United States has now surpassed levels seen during the Gilded Age, with a staggering half of all American children living in poverty. This unprecedented disparity is intensifying calls for systemic change, as grassroots movements and some policymakers push for wealth taxes and expanded social programs. The majority of analysts warn that such extreme inequality is unsustainable and risks fueling social unrest, especially as federal support for vulnerable populations remains uncertain amid ongoing political gridlock.

**Ukraine: Corruption Scandal Deepens Western Disillusionment**

Fresh scrutiny has emerged over financial misconduct within the Ukrainian government. **Larry C. Johnson** argues that recent revelations about President Volodymyr Zelensky’s administration are not merely “money laundering,” as often described in Western media, but outright theft. Johnson contends that this distinction matters, as it exposes a deeper level of criminality and mismanagement of Western aid. The consensus among independent analysts is that these corruption scandals are eroding Western public and political support for continued assistance to Ukraine, further complicating Kyiv’s position as Russian forces maintain their advantage.

**Venezuela: U.S. Intervention and Privatization Plans**

In Latin America, the U.S. continues to escalate its posture toward Venezuela, with new details emerging about Washington’s preferred post-coup leadership. **Chris Hedges** reports that María Corina Machado, a far-right figure funded by the U.S. government, is offering to sell $1.7 trillion of Venezuela’s assets to American corporations if installed as leader. Her open support for military intervention against her own country is seen by analysts as a sign of the lack of viable, broadly legitimate alternatives to the current government. The majority view is that U.S. intervention is likely to deepen instability and provoke backlash both within Venezuela and across the region.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trend is one of mounting disillusionment with established power structures, whether in the form of unchecked inequality in the U.S., endemic corruption in Ukraine, or foreign-backed regime change in Venezuela. Analysts broadly agree that absent significant policy shifts, these crises will intensify, with growing risks of social unrest, political backlash, and regional destabilization. The coming weeks are expected to bring further revelations and heightened tensions, as global actors struggle to manage the fallout from these interconnected challenges.
