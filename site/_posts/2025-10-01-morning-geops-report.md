As the Gaza conflict and U.S. domestic turmoil continue to dominate global headlines, the past 12 hours have brought fresh signs of deepening polarization, both within Western societies and across the international system. New developments highlight the erosion of traditional alliances, the rise of state authoritarianism, and the growing disconnect between official narratives and public sentiment.

**US-Israel “Peace Plan” Faces Widespread Rejection**

The White House’s latest Gaza ceasefire proposal, heavily revised to accommodate Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu’s demands, has been met with fierce opposition from Arab negotiators. **Ray McGovern** reports that Arab officials are “furious” over the changes, which they see as undermining trust in U.S. mediation and further entrenching Israeli control over Palestinian territories. This sentiment is echoed by **Caitlin Johnstone**, who warns that the so-called “Trumpanyahu” plan risks “cementing permanent subjugation of Palestinians,” reflecting a broader skepticism among analysts regarding the viability of U.S.-brokered peace initiatives. The plan’s rejection by both Palestinian representatives and hardline Israeli factions underscores the diplomatic deadlock and the diminishing influence of Washington in the region.

**US Public Opinion Shifts Dramatically on Israel-Palestine**

A new poll marks a historic shift in American attitudes: for the first time, more U.S. voters now support Palestinians than Israelis. **Ray McGovern** highlights this “stunning reversal,” attributing it to widespread disillusionment with Israel’s conduct in Gaza and growing awareness of the humanitarian crisis. Analysts suggest this shift could have significant implications for U.S. foreign policy, especially as the 2026 election cycle approaches and lawmakers face increasing pressure to reconsider unconditional support for Israel.

**Unprecedented US Military Leadership Meeting Raises Speculation**

In an unusual move, President Trump and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth convened over 800 U.S. General Officers at Quantico Marine Base for a closed-door meeting. **Larry C. Johnson** questions the necessity of such a gathering, suggesting it may have been intended to “distract from a more significant, secretive agenda” related to potential military operations or a shift in U.S. defense posture. The timing, amid escalating Middle East tensions and domestic unrest, has fueled speculation about possible preparations for new military actions or a recalibration of American strategy abroad.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of deepening mistrust—both of U.S. diplomatic efforts in the Middle East and of domestic institutions tasked with upholding democratic norms. The Gaza conflict continues to polarize Western societies, with public opinion increasingly at odds with official policy. The unprecedented military leadership meeting signals uncertainty about America’s next moves, while the collapse of U.S.-led peace efforts and the shift in public sentiment point toward a period of heightened volatility and unpredictability. Most analysts expect further diplomatic paralysis, rising domestic polarization, and a continued erosion of Western influence on the global stage in the coming days.
