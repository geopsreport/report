As the year closes, the global geopolitical environment is marked by persistent instability, with new developments in European defense, U.S. military policy, and the Middle East further complicating the outlook.

**Europe’s Defense Autonomy Falters as FCAS Stalls**

Europe’s ambition to achieve defense autonomy through the Future Combat Air System (FCAS) is facing a critical setback. According to **Daniel L. Davis**, the FCAS project—intended as a collaborative next-generation fighter initiative led by France, Germany, and Spain—is now at risk of collapse due to renewed disputes over governance and cost-sharing. This impasse threatens to leave NATO even more reliant on U.S. platforms, particularly the F-35, which, despite ongoing criticism over cost and control, remains the alliance’s most advanced and widely adopted fighter. Portugal’s decision to replace its F-16s with up to 28 F-35As, as confirmed by Air Force chief Gen. João Cartaxo Alves, underscores this trend. Meanwhile, Canada’s reconsideration of its F-35 purchase in favor of the Saab Gripen could create logistical and interoperability challenges for the Royal Canadian Air Force.

**U.S. Military Modernization: Battleship Revival and Missile Defense Ambitions**

In the United States, President Trump’s announcement of the “Trump-class” battleship program—marketed as the “Golden Fleet”—has sparked debate over its strategic value. **Daniel L. Davis** draws historical parallels to the Montana-class battleships, which were ultimately rendered obsolete by shifts in naval warfare. Analysts widely agree that any new battleship must be fundamentally reimagined, emphasizing networked warfare, missile capabilities, and advanced command-and-control systems to avoid repeating past mistakes. Simultaneously, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s “Golden Dome” initiative aims to establish a comprehensive, multi-layered missile defense system by 2028, integrating satellites, Aegis ships, drones, and ground sensors to counter both ballistic and hypersonic threats.

**Middle East: Israeli Settlement Expansion and Humanitarian Crisis in Gaza**

In the Middle East, Israel’s far-right government has approved the construction of 19 new settlements in the occupied West Bank, a move described by **Chris Hedges** as deepening the apartheid system and further undermining prospects for a viable Palestinian state. The humanitarian situation in Gaza continues to deteriorate, with hospitals pushed “past the point of failure” due to severe restrictions on medical aid and critical shortages of essential medicines, according to reports from Dr. Mimi Syed and Gaza’s Health Ministry.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trends point to growing fragmentation and risk. Europe’s inability to unify on defense procurement may entrench dependence on U.S. military technology. In the U.S., ambitious modernization projects face skepticism over their relevance and feasibility. In the Middle East, expanding Israeli settlements and the worsening humanitarian crisis in Gaza are likely to fuel further instability. Across all theaters, the lack of diplomatic breakthroughs and the acceleration of military-technological competition suggest that the risk of escalation and systemic crisis will persist into 2026.
