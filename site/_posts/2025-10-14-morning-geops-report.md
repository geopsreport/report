As the Gaza ceasefire enters its second week, the gap between official narratives and realities on the ground continues to widen, with new developments underscoring persistent instability in the Middle East and shifting dynamics in U.S. domestic and foreign policy.

**Gaza Ceasefire: Continued Violence and Political Theater**

Despite the formal signing of President Trump’s Gaza Peace Plan, skepticism remains widespread among analysts and observers. **Larry C. Johnson** characterizes the event as “a wedding without a bride and groom,” noting the conspicuous absence of both Israeli and Palestinian representatives and questioning the plan’s legitimacy and effectiveness. Meanwhile, Israeli military actions have not ceased; **Caitlin Johnstone** reports that Israeli officials are openly planning to resume attacks on Gaza once the remaining hostages are released, with Defense Minister Israel Katz emphasizing the intent to “destroy Hamas’s tunnels” and demilitarize the territory under U.S. oversight. On the ground, rescue workers have recovered over 200 Palestinian bodies from the rubble since the ceasefire began, highlighting the ongoing humanitarian crisis.

**U.S. Policy: Open Admissions and Media Influence**

In a rare public admission, President Trump has openly acknowledged his alignment with Israeli-American billionaire Miriam Adelson’s interests in shaping U.S. policy toward Israel. **Caitlin Johnstone** points out that this level of transparency about foreign-linked donor influence is unprecedented, especially considering the scrutiny Trump faced over alleged foreign interference during his first term. This comes amid broader concerns about the narrowing of dissenting voices in U.S. media and politics, with pro-Israel billionaires increasing their influence over major news outlets.

**Ukraine: Shifting Signals and Leadership Calculations**

On the Eastern front, the Ukraine conflict remains unresolved, but new signals are emerging. **Gilbert Doctorow** notes that President Trump’s statements about potential military support for Ukraine serve as a warning to President Putin, urging him to end the conflict. Doctorow draws a parallel between Putin’s current posture and Gorbachev’s past naivety in negotiations, suggesting that Moscow may be underestimating the risks of Western engagement.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to a deepening crisis of legitimacy for Western-led peace initiatives, with political spectacle and elite interests overshadowing substantive progress. In Gaza, the ceasefire is widely viewed as fragile and temporary, with both sides preparing for renewed conflict. The U.S. continues to project influence through both overt and covert means, but growing transparency about these mechanisms is fueling public skepticism. In Ukraine, leadership uncertainty and shifting Western signals may introduce new volatility. Absent meaningful engagement from the actual parties to the conflicts and genuine international oversight, analysts predict a return to violence in Gaza and further erosion of trust in diplomatic solutions globally.
