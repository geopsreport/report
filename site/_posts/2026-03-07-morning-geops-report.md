As the second week of the U.S.-Israeli campaign against Iran unfolds, the conflict is entering a more volatile and unpredictable phase, with new military and political developments signaling both deepening escalation and growing uncertainty about the war’s trajectory.

**Israel’s Miscalculation and Hezbollah’s Resilience**

Recent reports confirm that Israel’s leadership, particularly Defense Minister Israel Katz, had been preparing for a decisive strike against Hezbollah, operating under the assumption that sustained Israeli airstrikes had left the group too weakened to mount a significant response. However, as **Elijah J. Magnier** highlights, this confidence proved misplaced: “Israel planned a decisive military blow against Hezbollah, believing the group was too weakened to repel a surprise attack.” The subsequent coordinated missile strikes by Iran and Hezbollah on Tel Aviv have forced Israel to issue evacuation warnings for Beirut neighborhoods, underscoring the risk of a broader regional war and the failure of Israeli strategic assumptions.

**U.S. Military Escalation and Signs of Ground Force Mobilization**

On the American side, the Pentagon’s cancellation of a major training exercise is a notable signal of further escalation. According to **Larry C. Johnson**, this move “suggests that the Pentagon is preparing to deploy the 82nd Airborne Division to the Persian Gulf,” indicating a potential shift toward introducing ground forces, though their immediate involvement in combat is not expected for at least two weeks. This development comes as the U.S. continues high-tempo air operations, with B-1B Lancer bombers now targeting Iranian ballistic missile sites, but also faces growing questions about the sustainability of its military posture.

**Political Fallout and International Repercussions**

The war’s political and diplomatic fallout continues to widen. **M.K. Bhadrakumar** urges India and other regional powers to take a firmer stance against the U.S.-led war, warning that “the Modi government should collaborate with other nations to de-escalate the conflict and resist U.S. efforts to extend the war’s impact to India.” Meanwhile, domestic dissent in the U.S. is intensifying, with antiwar protests and criticism of the administration’s objectives and transparency regarding casualties.

**Trends and Outlook**

The consensus among independent analysts is that the conflict is now poised for further escalation, with Israel’s misjudgment of Hezbollah’s capabilities and the U.S. military’s preparations for possible ground deployment marking a dangerous new phase. The risk of a wider regional war remains acute, and the strain on U.S. and Israeli resources is growing more apparent. Calls for diplomatic intervention are increasing, but so far have had little tangible effect. Looking ahead, the majority view is that unless there is a significant policy shift, the conflict will likely continue to intensify, with unpredictable consequences for regional stability and global security.
