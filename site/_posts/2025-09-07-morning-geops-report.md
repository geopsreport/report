As the international order faces mounting strain, the last 12 hours have brought new revelations on the Gaza crisis, the evolving Eurasian alliance, and the shifting landscape of U.S. foreign policy, with independent analysts highlighting deepening divides and growing skepticism toward Western leadership.

**Gaza: U.S. Sanctions Palestinian Human Rights Groups Amid Genocide Accusations**

The humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza continues to draw global outrage, with the U.S. State Department—under Secretary Marco Rubio—now imposing sanctions on three major Palestinian human rights organizations. According to **Chris Hedges**, these groups are being targeted for their involvement in the International Criminal Court’s case against Israeli officials, a move widely criticized as an attempt to undermine accountability efforts. This comes as the International Association of Genocide Scholars’ resolution—endorsed by 86% of its members—declares Israel’s actions in Gaza as genocide, further isolating the Netanyahu government. **Jeffrey Sachs** argues that “America’s complicity” is now a central focus of international criticism, with the U.S. increasingly seen as an obstacle to humanitarian intervention. Meanwhile, the Global Sumud Flotilla’s mission to break the siege on Gaza faces new threats, as Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir seeks to label activists as terrorists, according to **Caitlin Johnstone**.

**Eurasian Realignment: SCO Summit and Shifting Power Dynamics**

The Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) summit continues to shape Eurasian geopolitics, with new insights from a discussion between **Gilbert Doctorow** and Professor Glenn Diesen. The summit’s outcomes underscore a deepening partnership between Russia, China, and India, united in opposition to U.S. hegemony. The analysts highlight the significance of the Beijing military parade and ongoing energy projects, such as Mongolia’s role in the Power of Siberia-2 pipeline, as evidence of growing regional integration. There is also speculation about potential political fallout in Europe, with a prediction that European Parliament President Ursula von der Leyen may soon face a major setback, reflecting broader instability within the EU.

**NATO and Russia: Historical Roots of Today’s Rift**

A newly resurfaced video from a 2001 press conference between Vladimir Putin and George W. Bush, discussed by **Larry C. Johnson**, sheds light on the origins of the current Russia-NATO divide. Putin’s concerns about NATO expansion and references to a Soviet proposal for European security were dismissed by Bush, which Johnson argues marked the beginning of a widening rift between Russia and the West. This historical context is increasingly relevant as the Ukraine conflict drags on and NATO’s cohesion is tested by both military and economic pressures.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to accelerating polarization: the U.S. faces mounting criticism for its role in the Gaza crisis, while its attempts to sanction Palestinian human rights groups are seen as efforts to stifle accountability. In Eurasia, the SCO summit marks a consolidation of multipolar alliances, with Russia, China, and India deepening cooperation across security and energy sectors. The resurfacing of historical grievances between Russia and NATO underscores the long-standing roots of current tensions, suggesting that diplomatic solutions will require a fundamental reassessment of past missteps.

Looking ahead, analysts expect further international action in support of Palestinian rights, continued realignment among Eurasian powers, and persistent challenges to Western influence in global governance. The risk of escalation remains high, but so too does the potential for new diplomatic openings—if key actors are willing to engage.
