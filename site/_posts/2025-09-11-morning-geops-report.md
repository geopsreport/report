As the international system continues to fracture along new geopolitical lines, the last 12 hours have brought significant developments in Western political scandals, the ongoing crisis in Gaza, and the evolving landscape of global alliances.

**UK Parliamentary Scandal: Israel Funding Prompts Westminster Group Shutdown**

A major political scandal has erupted in the UK, where a cross-party parliamentary group was disbanded after revelations it received funding from an Israeli-owned weapons company. **Ray McGovern** reports that this shutdown underscores growing concerns about foreign influence in Western democracies, particularly regarding Israel’s role in shaping policy through financial channels. The incident has fueled debate in the UK over transparency, lobbying, and the integrity of parliamentary decision-making at a time when public scrutiny of foreign entanglements is intensifying.

**United Nations at 80: Paralysis Amid Gaza Crisis**

As the United Nations marks its 80th anniversary, its inability to address the Gaza conflict has come under renewed criticism. **Vijay Prashad** highlights the UN’s “structural limitations and political divisions,” which have rendered it largely ineffective in halting violence or brokering a durable ceasefire. Analysts broadly agree that the Security Council’s deadlock, exacerbated by U.S. vetoes and great power rivalry, has left the UN sidelined during one of the most acute humanitarian crises in recent memory. This has reinforced perceptions of the UN’s declining relevance in managing global security.

**Eurasian Realignment and the Rise of Multilateralism**

The aftermath of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) summit continues to reshape global alliances. **Michael Hudson** notes that the Tianjin meeting marked a decisive step toward the formation of two competing blocs, with the “Global Majority” seeking to free themselves from U.S. economic dominance. The consolidation of the SCO, alongside BRICS, is seen as a defensive response to Western sanctions and trade wars, particularly as the U.S. intensifies its focus on Europe and Asia. The majority view among analysts is that this realignment is accelerating the decline of Western hegemony and fostering a more multipolar world order.

**Trends and Predictions**

The dominant trends point to deepening mistrust of Western political institutions, as scandals over foreign funding and lobbying come to light. The UN’s inability to address major crises like Gaza is fueling calls for reform or alternative mechanisms for conflict resolution. Meanwhile, the rise of Eurasian-led multilateral organizations signals a continued erosion of Western influence and the emergence of new power centers. Looking ahead, analysts expect further fragmentation of the international system, with increased scrutiny of political funding, persistent paralysis at the UN, and a steady shift toward multipolarity in global affairs.
