As the last week of February 2026 begins, the global order is marked by mounting military, technological, and economic uncertainty, with new developments in the U.S.-Iran standoff, the Ukraine war, and the evolving landscape of strategic air power.

**U.S.-Iran: Military Buildup and Strategic Timing**

The risk of U.S. military action against Iran remains acute, with the situation entering a phase of strategic calculation. **Larry C. Johnson** highlights that while President Trump has not yet authorized strikes, the significant buildup of U.S. combat air assets in the region continues, and “the timing of any air operation may be influenced by the lunar cycle, with moonless nights in mid-March seen as optimal for stealth operations.” This suggests that the current pause may be less a de-escalation than a tactical delay, as both Washington and Tel Aviv weigh the risks and opportunities of a potential attack. The consensus among analysts is that the window for diplomacy is rapidly narrowing, with the possibility of a multi-front conflict remaining high.

**Ukraine War: Tactical Evolution and Russian Setbacks**

On the Eastern front, the Ukraine war is driving a profound transformation in military doctrine. **Anatol Lieven** notes that the conflict has “marked the biggest evolution in military tactics since WWII,” with drones and layered defensive strategies now central to the battlefield. This shift has not only shaped the course of the war but also has broader implications for future conflicts, particularly in the context of potential U.S.-China tensions over Taiwan. Meanwhile, **Daniel L. Davis** underscores that Russia’s Su-57 “Felon” stealth fighter, despite its advanced features, “has zero chance of ever challenging NATO” due to limited production and technological gaps, further illustrating Russia’s strategic difficulties as the war enters its fourth year.

**China and Russia: Strategic Bomber Ambitions and Potential Collaboration**

In Asia, China’s unveiling of the Xian H-20 stealth bomber is prompting a reassessment of global air power. **Daniel L. Davis** reports that the H-20’s emergence has shifted the debate to the scale of China’s future bomber fleet, with the U.S. unable to match the projected numbers. Simultaneously, both Russia and China are pursuing next-generation stealth bombers—the H-20 and the PAK DA, respectively—but face significant technical and production challenges. Davis raises the prospect of a future joint bomber project, stating, “the possibility of a collaborative effort between Russia and China cannot be ruled out,” which would further complicate the strategic balance for the U.S. and its allies.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of intensifying arms competition and strategic brinkmanship. The U.S.-Iran confrontation is in a precarious holding pattern, with military preparations continuing and diplomatic options dwindling. In Ukraine, the evolution of warfare is accelerating, but Russia’s inability to close the technological gap with NATO is becoming more apparent. China’s rapid progress in strategic bomber development, and the potential for Sino-Russian collaboration, signal a long-term challenge to U.S. air dominance. As the majority of analysts caution, the coming weeks are likely to see further escalation risks, with global stability hinging on the ability of leaders to manage overlapping crises and avoid miscalculation.
