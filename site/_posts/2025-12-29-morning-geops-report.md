As 2025 draws to a close, the global security environment is marked by intensifying military competition, evolving intelligence operations, and persistent regional crises. The latest independent analyses highlight new developments in airpower training, covert influence campaigns, and the ongoing contest for dominance in the international arms market.

**China’s Air Combat Training Targets Western Fighters**

China is signaling growing confidence in its air force by publicizing rare footage of war-gaming exercises simulating air combat between its J-16 fighters and France’s Dassault Rafale. **Daniel L. Davis** notes that this move reflects “increased confidence in its military training and strategy,” as the People’s Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) adapts to potential Western adversaries beyond the U.S. F-22 and F-35. While the results of these exercises remain undisclosed, the focus on the Rafale—regarded as one of the world’s most versatile 4.5-generation fighters—suggests Beijing is preparing for a broader range of scenarios in the Indo-Pacific and beyond.

**Intelligence Operations and Lawfare in Europe**

New leaks reported by **Max Blumenthal** reveal that the Commission for International Justice and Accountability (CIJA), a Western government-funded entity, attempted to infiltrate and intimidate EU financial regulators after facing corruption allegations. The documents also expose CIJA’s collaboration with al-Qaeda-linked groups in Syria, raising serious questions about the integrity of Western-backed legal and intelligence operations. Analysts view these revelations as further evidence of the blurred lines between lawfare, covert action, and influence operations in the European theater, complicating efforts to maintain transparency and rule of law.

**Arms Market: F-35 Maintains Edge Over Chinese Rivals**

Despite China’s push to modernize its air force and promote the J-35A stealth fighter internationally, the U.S.-made F-35 continues to dominate global fighter sales. **Daniel L. Davis** reports that “countries continue to prefer the F-35 over [the J-35A],” with the Chinese platform struggling to secure international buyers. This persistent preference underscores the enduring technological and logistical advantages of the F-35, as well as the strength of U.S. defense partnerships, even as China seeks to expand its influence through arms exports and technology transfer.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of deepening competition across both conventional and unconventional domains. China’s focus on realistic air combat training and the West’s reliance on intelligence-driven lawfare reflect a shift toward more sophisticated and multi-layered forms of rivalry. Meanwhile, the U.S. retains a clear advantage in the global arms market, but faces ongoing challenges from emerging technologies and shifting alliances.

Looking ahead, analysts expect further escalation in information and influence operations, continued modernization of air and naval forces, and persistent volatility in regions where covert and overt power struggles intersect. The consensus is that 2026 will see these trends intensify, with few signs of diplomatic or institutional breakthroughs on the horizon.
