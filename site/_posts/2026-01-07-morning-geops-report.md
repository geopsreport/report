As the global order reels from the shockwaves of the U.S. operation in Venezuela, the past 12 hours have brought further escalation and new signals of great-power confrontation. Independent analysts highlight Russia’s targeted retaliation, renewed scrutiny of U.S. legal justifications, and the expanding reach of China’s missile forces—each development deepening the sense of instability and unpredictability.

**Russian Strikes on U.S. Interests in Ukraine**

In a direct response to the U.S. abduction of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, Russia has launched missile strikes on American-owned facilities in Ukraine. **Larry C. Johnson** reports that the Oleina oil extraction plant in Dnipro and the Flex manufacturing site in Mukachevo were both severely damaged, with the attacks widely interpreted as a pointed message to President Trump. Johnson also notes that the operation against Maduro was enabled by betrayal within his own circle, raising further questions about the security of Venezuela’s interim leadership and the broader risks of internal destabilization.

**Mounting Legal and Political Fallout Over Venezuela**

The legality of the U.S. raid on Venezuela is facing intensifying global scrutiny. **Marjorie Cohn** argues that the Trump administration’s claims of U.S. ownership over Venezuelan oil are “untenable under international law,” and the operation itself is a clear violation of the UN Charter. This sentiment is echoed across independent outlets, with analysts warning that the precedent of forcibly removing a sitting head of state without UN approval marks a dangerous erosion of international norms. **Jack Rasmus** points to a broader trend of lawlessness, noting the increasing use of paramilitary and special operations units for unlawful actions both domestically and abroad, and the weaponization of legal systems for political ends.

**China’s Expanding Missile Threat in the Pacific**

While attention remains fixed on Latin America and Eastern Europe, the strategic balance in the Pacific is also shifting rapidly. **Daniel L. Davis** highlights that China’s DF-27 missile—a conventionally armed ICBM with an anti-ship variant—now poses a credible threat to U.S. Navy aircraft carriers as far as Alaska and Hawaii. The Pentagon’s latest China Military Power Report, cited by Davis, underscores that China’s Rocket Force can target U.S. naval assets nearly anywhere in the Pacific, including Guam and Pearl Harbor. This development is prompting urgent debate in Washington about the survivability of carrier groups and the need for new naval strategies.

**Trends and Predictions**

The consensus among independent analysts is that the U.S. operation in Venezuela has triggered a dangerous new phase of international instability, with both Russia and China signaling their willingness to challenge American power projection. The normalization of extrajudicial measures and disregard for international law are fueling a climate of unpredictability and risk. Looking ahead, the risk of escalation remains acute: further Russian retaliation, potential U.S. or allied countermeasures, and the ongoing transformation of military balances in both Latin America and the Pacific suggest that the coming weeks will be pivotal for global security and the future of the international order.
