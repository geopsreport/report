As the Middle East remains in a state of uneasy calm following the U.S.-Israeli campaign against Iran, the last 12 hours have brought fresh signs of both diplomatic maneuvering and persistent instability, with regional and international actors recalibrating their strategies amid ongoing uncertainty.

**Saudi Arabia Signals Openness to Turkish Mediation**

In a notable shift, Saudi Arabia has issued a statement welcoming “constructive regional dialogue” in response to Turkey’s recent proposal for a Gulf security summit. **Abdulkhaleq Abdulla** observes that “Riyadh’s measured endorsement reflects a pragmatic recognition that regional solutions may now be more effective than reliance on Western mediation.” While concrete commitments remain pending, this marks the first public indication that Saudi leaders are willing to engage in Ankara’s initiative, potentially opening the door to broader talks involving Iran and Egypt.

**Iranian Oil Exports Resume, but Under Tight Controls**

Following Iran’s announcement of a conditional reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, new shipping data indicates that Iranian oil exports have resumed on a limited basis, primarily to Asian buyers. **Esfandyar Batmanghelidj** notes that “Tehran is tightly regulating outbound shipments, prioritizing contracts with partners who have signaled political support during the crisis.” The U.S. blockade remains in effect, and insurance premiums for tankers transiting the strait are still elevated, underscoring the fragility of the current arrangement.

**Lebanon Ceasefire Monitors Face Access Challenges**

On the ground in Lebanon, international ceasefire monitors are reporting difficulty accessing several areas in southern Lebanon due to ongoing security concerns and damaged infrastructure. **Mona Yacoubian** reports that “without reliable access, it is nearly impossible to verify compliance or deliver humanitarian aid to displaced populations.” This development raises further doubts about the sustainability of the ceasefire and the prospects for meaningful recovery in affected regions.

**Trends and Outlook**

The latest developments suggest a cautious but growing willingness among regional powers to explore homegrown diplomatic solutions, with Turkey’s mediation initiative gaining incremental traction. However, the situation remains highly volatile: Iranian oil exports are vulnerable to renewed disruptions, and the Lebanon ceasefire is increasingly difficult to enforce on the ground. Most analysts agree that absent sustained diplomatic engagement and tangible progress on security and humanitarian issues, the risk of renewed conflict and economic instability remains high. The coming days will be critical in determining whether regional actors can translate tentative dialogue into lasting de-escalation.
