As the Iran conflict grinds into its sixth week, the war’s ripple effects are increasingly visible in military, economic, and political arenas, with fresh developments highlighting both the fragility of Western defense initiatives and the global economic order.

**European Defense Crisis: FCAS on the Brink**

Europe’s ambitions for military autonomy are facing a critical test as the Future Combat Air System (FCAS) program, the continent’s flagship sixth-generation fighter project, teeters on the edge of collapse. **Daniel L. Davis** warns that “Europe has 2-3 weeks to save its 6th-generation fighter program,” citing unresolved leadership disputes between France and Germany. The failure of FCAS would not only undermine Europe’s defense industrial base but also deepen the continent’s reliance on U.S. technology at a time when transatlantic ties are under unprecedented strain due to the Iran war.

**U.S. Military Modernization: Carrier and Fighter Gaps**

The operational strain on U.S. forces in the Gulf is exposing deep-seated challenges in military modernization. While the USS Gerald R. Ford has returned to duty after repairs, the Navy’s next-generation F/A-XX stealth fighter program is faltering, receiving only $74 million in funding for FY 2026 compared to the Air Force’s $5 billion boost for its F-47 program. **Davis** notes that “the Navy is in big trouble without the F/A-XX,” as delays and underfunding threaten to leave carrier air wings reliant on aging platforms. Meanwhile, Air Force leaders are questioning whether the planned fleet of 100 B-21 Raider bombers will be sufficient to maintain air superiority against adversaries with advanced air defenses.

**Strait of Hormuz and Global Economic Risks**

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz remains a central flashpoint, but analysts are increasingly warning that the global economic fallout could be even more severe if other chokepoints are threatened. **Davis** highlights the strategic significance of the Strait of Malacca, through which 80% of China’s oil passes, arguing that while Hormuz can “shock the global economy,” Malacca could “stop it cold.” With oil prices still surging and the U.S. unable to force open Hormuz, the risk of cascading disruptions to global trade and energy flows is mounting.

**Trends and Outlook**

The consensus across sources is that the Iran conflict has triggered a wider crisis of confidence in Western defense planning and economic resilience. Europe’s FCAS program faces imminent collapse, U.S. naval aviation modernization is lagging, and the world economy remains vulnerable to further shocks from maritime disruptions. Analysts expect continued military and economic uncertainty, with the potential for further escalation if diplomatic solutions remain out of reach. The coming weeks will be pivotal for both European defense cooperation and the stability of global supply chains, as the war’s indirect consequences become ever more pronounced.
