As the global order remains in flux, the past 12 hours have seen notable developments in the Middle East, U.S. domestic politics, and international diplomacy, reflecting the deepening crises and shifting alliances that define the current geopolitical landscape.

**Bernie Sanders Breaks Silence on Gaza, Draws Criticism**

After two years of public silence, U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders has finally acknowledged the ongoing genocide in Gaza. However, as **Caitlin Johnstone** reports, Sanders’ statement has sparked controversy for “beginning by blaming Hamas for the violence and framing Israel’s actions as self-defense.” This approach has drawn sharp criticism from both progressive and Palestinian advocates, who argue that it downplays the severity of Israel’s campaign and echoes official U.S. narratives. The episode highlights the growing divide within the Democratic Party and among Western progressives over the Gaza conflict, as well as the broader challenge of confronting U.S. policy on Israel.

**Iran-IAEA Cairo Agreement: A Diplomatic Opening Amid Regional Turmoil**

In a rare positive diplomatic development, Iran and the International Atomic Energy Agency have signed the Cairo Agreement, which allows for renewed inspections of Iranian nuclear facilities and establishes new transparency measures. According to **Larry C. Johnson**, the deal is “designed to build trust and provide a framework for ongoing verification,” signaling a willingness by Tehran to engage with international norms despite regional tensions. Analysts note that while the agreement could ease immediate nuclear concerns, its impact may be limited by the broader instability triggered by the Gaza conflict and Israel’s regional actions.

**U.S. Supreme Court Expands Police Powers, Civil Liberties Advocates Alarmed**

Domestically, the U.S. Supreme Court has issued a ruling permitting police to demand identification from individuals without probable cause. **Judge Andrew Napolitano**, cited by **Ray McGovern**, questioned the muted response from civil liberties advocates, warning that the decision “defies the Fourth Amendment” and sets a dangerous precedent for government overreach. This ruling, combined with the advance of the “Charlie Kirk Act,” is seen by analysts as part of a broader trend toward the erosion of constitutional protections and the normalization of state surveillance.

**Trends and Outlook**

The latest developments underscore the hardening of political and ideological fault lines both within the U.S. and internationally. The Gaza conflict continues to polarize Western societies and expose divisions among political leaders, while diplomatic efforts such as the Iran-IAEA agreement offer only limited relief amid widespread instability. The expansion of police powers and speech restrictions in the U.S. reflects a broader authoritarian drift, with civil liberties increasingly at risk. Most analysts expect these trends to persist, with escalating regional conflicts, deepening domestic polarization, and a continued shift toward multipolarity on the global stage.
