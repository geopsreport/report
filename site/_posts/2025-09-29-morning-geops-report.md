As the international order continues to fracture, the past 12 hours have brought new developments in U.S. domestic politics, Western policy shifts, and the ongoing fallout from Israel’s regional military actions—each highlighting deepening polarization and institutional strain.

**Trump Administration’s Expansion of Federal Powers and Political Retaliation**

In the United States, President Trump’s administration has further expanded federal authority to target individuals or groups accused of promoting “political violence.” **Caitlin Johnstone** notes that the new national security directive “identifies targets based on various ideological criteria,” with a particular focus on leftist groups, despite evidence that many violent acts have been committed by U.S. military veterans. This move is widely seen by analysts as part of a broader trend toward authoritarianism and increased state control over dissent. Simultaneously, the indictment of former FBI Director James Comey for allegedly making false statements to Congress is drawing scrutiny. **Aaron Maté** argues that Trump’s pressure on the Justice Department to pursue charges against Comey, and the replacement of a reluctant U.S. attorney with a loyalist, “reflects the controversial standards of the 2016 Trump-Russia investigation” and is viewed by many as politically motivated retaliation.

**UK Digital ID Mandate and Broader Western Surveillance Trends**

In the UK, Prime Minister Keir Starmer’s announcement of mandatory digital IDs for employment has sparked debate over civil liberties. **Caitlin Johnstone** contends that such measures “are not driven by public demand but rather serve governmental interests,” reflecting a wider Western trend toward digital surveillance and control. Critics warn that these policies could further erode privacy and individual autonomy, aligning with similar authoritarian shifts seen in the U.S.

**Israel’s Regional Escalation and Diplomatic Fallout**

On the international front, the repercussions of Israel’s recent airstrikes in Yemen continue to reverberate. **Chris Hedges** reports that the strikes, which killed at least nine civilians in Sanaa, represent one of Israel’s largest operations in Yemen and have intensified scrutiny of its military campaign beyond Gaza. The attacks, justified by Israeli officials as necessary to counter Houthi threats, have drawn condemnation for their civilian toll and are seen as exacerbating regional instability. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Netanyahu’s hardline rhetoric at the UN and rejection of a two-state solution have further isolated Israel diplomatically, with more countries signaling support for Palestinian statehood.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trends point to growing authoritarianism and polarization in Western democracies, with expanded state powers and politicized justice raising alarms about the future of civil liberties. In the UK and U.S., surveillance and control measures are likely to face mounting legal and public resistance. Internationally, Israel’s widening military campaign and diplomatic isolation are expected to fuel further instability in the Middle East, while the erosion of Western influence accelerates the shift toward a more fragmented global order. The coming days are likely to bring intensified scrutiny of both domestic and foreign policy actions, with analysts predicting continued volatility and a widening gap between official narratives and public sentiment.
