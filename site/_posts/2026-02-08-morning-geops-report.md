As global power dynamics remain unsettled, the past half-day has brought new developments in North American defense procurement, U.S. military modernization, and the personal fallout from elite controversies, all reflecting broader trends of strategic uncertainty and shifting alliances.

**Canada’s F-35 Deal Falters Amid Rising U.S. Tensions**

Diplomatic friction between Ottawa and Washington is threatening to upend Canada’s long-standing plan to acquire F-35 fighter jets. According to **Daniel L. Davis**, Prime Minister Mark Carney is now “considering reducing the country’s F-35 order in favor of Sweden’s Saab Gripen E/F,” with the Gripen’s Mach 2 speed outpacing the F-35’s Mach 1.6 and emerging as a key factor for Canadian defense planners. This potential pivot is driven by both operational concerns and a desire to assert greater sovereignty, as well as unease over the costs and interoperability requirements of the U.S.-centric F-35 program. Should the deal collapse, analysts warn it could have ripple effects across NORAD and NATO, emboldening other allies to reconsider U.S.-led procurement frameworks.

**U.S. Military Modernization: Lessons from the Past and Future Risks**

The U.S. military’s struggle to balance legacy platforms with next-generation capabilities is again in focus. **Daniel L. Davis** draws attention to the historical example of the XB-70 Valkyrie, a Mach 3 bomber whose ambitious design ultimately proved unsustainable, serving as “a cautionary tale for the U.S. Air Force.” This reflection comes as the Air Force faces similar dilemmas with the high costs and uncertain future of platforms like the B-21 Raider and the F-47 NGAD fighter. The ongoing debate underscores concerns that technological ambition, if not matched by affordability and practicality, could undermine long-term force readiness.

**Elite Accountability: Chomsky-Epstein Controversy Resurfaces**

On the societal front, the controversy surrounding Noam Chomsky’s past friendship with Jeffrey Epstein has resurfaced, with new statements from Chomsky’s wife Valeria. **Aaron Maté** reports that, given Chomsky’s severe health issues following a 2023 stroke, Valeria has taken the unusual step of publicly addressing the matter, emphasizing her focus on his care and their lack of public relations resources. The episode highlights the ongoing scrutiny of elite networks and the challenges of public accountability, even as direct political consequences remain limited.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of fragmentation and reassessment. Canada’s reconsideration of its F-35 purchase signals growing skepticism among U.S. allies about the costs and strategic value of American defense partnerships. Within the U.S. military, the push for cutting-edge platforms is tempered by historical lessons about overreach and sustainability. Meanwhile, public trust in elite figures continues to erode amid unresolved controversies. Looking ahead, analysts expect further realignment in alliance structures, increased scrutiny of defense spending, and persistent questions about the credibility of both institutions and individuals at the heart of global power.
