As the US-Israeli war on Iran grinds into its third week, the conflict’s ripple effects continue to reshape military, diplomatic, and economic landscapes far beyond the immediate region. New developments highlight the mounting strain on Western military assets, growing fractures within allied coalitions, and the intensifying global energy crisis.

**European Fractures: Belgian PM Calls for Rapprochement with Russia**

A significant diplomatic rift has emerged within Europe as Belgian Prime Minister Bart de Wever publicly called for the normalization of relations with Russia and the resumption of energy imports. As reported by **Anatol Lieven**, de Wever’s stance is a direct response to the “ongoing Israeli-U.S. conflict with Iran, which is driving up energy prices and threatening Europe’s economic stability.” The move has drawn sharp criticism from other EU leaders, who remain committed to a hard line against Moscow. This development underscores deepening anxiety across Europe over energy security and the economic fallout of the war, as oil prices remain above $120 per barrel.

**US Navy Faces Critical Strike Capacity Gap**

The conflict is exposing vulnerabilities in US naval power projection. **Isaac Seitz** warns that the simultaneous retirement of four Ohio-class SSGNs and Ticonderoga-class cruisers is creating a “critical shortfall in the U.S. Navy’s long-range strike capability,” with no immediate replacement available. The Virginia-class Block V submarines, intended to fill the gap, will not be ready until the early 2030s. This capacity crunch is compounded by the decision to keep aging vessels like the USS Nimitz and Ohio-class submarines in service longer than planned, reflecting the acute operational demands of the current conflict.

**Iran’s Resilience and the Limits of Air Power**

Despite sustained US and Israeli air campaigns, Iran’s leadership and military infrastructure remain largely intact. **Kris Osborn** details how B-1B Lancer bombers have targeted Iranian missile production facilities, yet the overall impact has been limited by Iran’s ability to disperse and harden its assets. Meanwhile, **Elijah J. Magnier** emphasizes that the war, initially envisioned as a quick regime-change operation, has instead revealed the resilience of Iranian command structures and the limitations of Western air power in achieving decisive outcomes.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of strategic deadlock and mounting systemic strain. Europe’s internal divisions over energy policy signal a weakening of allied unity, while the US Navy’s strike gap raises questions about the sustainability of American force projection. Iran’s ability to withstand air assaults and maintain its deterrent posture further complicates prospects for a quick resolution. Most analysts anticipate continued attritional warfare, deepening economic disruption, and the risk of further regional or global escalation as diplomatic solutions remain elusive.
