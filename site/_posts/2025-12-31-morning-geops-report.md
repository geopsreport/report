As 2025 ends, the global landscape is defined by rapid military innovation, persistent regional instability, and mounting internal pressures in key states. The latest analyses highlight fresh developments in Iran’s domestic unrest, the intensifying U.S.–China arms race, and Russia’s efforts to expand its defense exports.

**Iran: Nationwide Protests and Western Hopes**

Widespread protests have erupted across Iran, triggered by economic hardship and a sharply devalued currency. According to **Larry C. Johnson**, the unrest has drawn significant attention from Western media and neoconservative circles, who view the demonstrations as a potential existential threat to the Islamic Republic. However, Johnson cautions that while the protests are serious, “the regime has weathered similar storms before,” suggesting that Western optimism about imminent regime change may be premature. The government’s response remains forceful, and the situation is fluid, but most analysts agree that the regime’s core security apparatus is still intact.

**U.S.–China: Sixth-Generation Fighter Race and Persistent Engine Gap**

The U.S.–China military rivalry continues to escalate, particularly in the field of next-generation airpower. China’s J-36 and J-50 sixth-generation stealth fighters are now confirmed to be in flight testing, with a third J-36 prototype recently spotted alongside a J-10C, as reported by **Daniel L. Davis**. Despite these advances, Davis emphasizes that “China continues to face significant challenges in developing advanced propulsion systems,” a technical hurdle that could delay their fighters’ full operational deployment. Meanwhile, the U.S. is pressing ahead with the F-47 NGAD, already in production, and is also rolling out major upgrades to the F-22 to maintain its edge against evolving threats.

**Russia: Su-57E Fighter Export Push to India**

In the international arms market, Russia is ramping up efforts to sell its Su-57E “Felon” stealth fighter to India. **Daniel L. Davis** reports that Moscow is offering an unprecedented package: local assembly, off-the-shelf deliveries, and even access to source code for the aircraft’s systems. This aggressive pitch reflects Russia’s need to secure major export deals amid ongoing sanctions and the shifting global defense landscape. Indian interest is significant, but analysts note that concerns about technology transfer and operational reliability remain.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of intensifying technological competition and internal pressures. The U.S. and China are locked in a high-stakes race for air dominance, with the U.S. currently leading in production timelines but China rapidly closing the gap. Iran’s domestic unrest adds a volatile new variable to Middle East dynamics, though regime change appears unlikely in the near term. Russia’s push to expand arms exports signals both economic necessity and a desire to maintain influence in Asia.

Looking ahead, analysts expect continued instability in Iran, further demonstrations of advanced military technology from both the U.S. and China, and increased competition for major defense contracts. The risk of sudden escalations remains high, particularly as domestic and international pressures intersect in unpredictable ways.
