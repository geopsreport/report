As geopolitical tensions escalate across the Middle East and Eurasia, the past 12 hours have brought new developments in Lebanon’s political crisis, the lead-up to the Trump-Putin summit, and the intensifying debate over U.S. policy toward Israel and Palestine.

**Lebanon: Disarmament Pressures and Sovereignty Risks**

Lebanon’s fragile political landscape faces mounting strain as foreign powers intensify demands for Hezbollah’s disarmament. According to **Elijah J. Magnier**, the so-called “Barrack Document”—a U.S.-Israeli blueprint—pressures Lebanese leaders to prioritize Hezbollah’s disarmament before any Israeli withdrawal or security guarantees. Magnier warns that this “take it or leave it” framework offers “no concrete concessions in return” from Israel and risks undermining Lebanon’s national defense and sovereignty. The majority of analysts agree that such external dictates are deepening Lebanon’s internal divisions and could destabilize the region further.

**Trump-Putin Summit: Historic Symbolism, Limited Optimism**

Anticipation is building for the upcoming Trump-Putin summit in Alaska, with independent observers highlighting both its symbolic weight and the prevailing skepticism. **Joe Lauria** describes the meeting as “historically significant,” while noting that expectations for a breakthrough remain low. Echoing this sentiment, **Tony Kevin** observes that both advocates for peace and proponents of continued confrontation share a sense of “gloom and anxiety” about the summit’s prospects. The consensus among analysts is that the summit is unlikely to produce substantive progress, serving more as a stage for political posturing amid persistent security concerns.

**U.S. Policy and Narratives: Double Standards and Religious Rhetoric**

The debate over U.S. policy in the Middle East has sharpened, with new attention on perceived double standards and the role of religious rhetoric. **Larry C. Johnson** highlights the U.S. government’s willingness to engage with Syria’s Hay’at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), a group with a record of violence, while justifying a hardline stance against Palestinian groups like Hamas. Johnson argues this reflects a “hypocrisy in U.S. foreign policy” that undermines Washington’s credibility. Meanwhile, **Caitlin Johnstone** reports on Senator Lindsey Graham’s recent speech, in which he warned that ending U.S. support for Israel would risk “divine retribution,” framing the alliance as a moral obligation for Americans and Christians. This religious framing, analysts note, is increasingly used to justify policy decisions and suppress dissent.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to escalating external pressures on Lebanon, with the potential for further internal fragmentation and regional instability. The Trump-Putin summit, while historic, is widely expected to yield little beyond symbolic gestures, reflecting entrenched divisions. In the U.S., the convergence of religious rhetoric and selective engagement with foreign actors is fueling criticism of official narratives and policy inconsistencies. Looking ahead, analysts predict continued polarization, with grassroots and international advocacy likely to play a growing role in shaping responses to these crises.
