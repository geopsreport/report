As the US-Israeli campaign in Iran grinds into its third week, the conflict’s operational, political, and economic reverberations are intensifying, with new developments highlighting deepening vulnerabilities for the US and its allies, as well as mounting global instability.

**US Missile Defense Shortage and Strategic Risk**

A critical new concern is the acute shortage of US missile defense systems. **Larry C. Johnson** reports that President Trump’s recent claim of “unlimited” air-defense missiles, particularly the PAC-3 MSE, is “simply not true,” with inventories running dangerously low after the opening salvos of Operation Epic Fury. This shortage, Johnson warns, “could leave US forces exposed not only in the Middle East but also in other theaters, especially if a second crisis erupts in the Indo-Pacific.” The depletion of Tomahawk missiles and the looming retirement of Ohio-class submarines further compound these vulnerabilities, raising alarms about the sustainability of US military operations.

**China’s Strategic Advantage and Shifting Naval Power**

The strain on US resources is being closely watched by global competitors. **Daniel L. Davis** emphasizes that China’s new aircraft carrier fleet, backed by advanced Anti-Access/Area-Denial (A2/AD) systems, now poses a significant challenge to US naval dominance. “The era of American carrier diplomacy is waning,” Davis writes, as Chinese capabilities increasingly limit the operational freedom of US supercarriers. This shift is particularly concerning given the US Navy’s current missile shortages and reduced vertical launch capacity, potentially emboldening China in the Taiwan Strait and beyond.

**European Paralysis and Political Fallout**

Meanwhile, Europe’s response to the Iran war is coming under renewed scrutiny. **Anatol Lieven** argues that European leaders’ “weak response” to US and Israeli actions is not only eroding their global influence but also fueling domestic political crises. The lack of a unified or forceful stance, Lieven warns, is “radicalizing politics at home,” with the risk of empowering extremist factions and undermining democratic institutions across the continent. This internal instability further limits Europe’s ability to act as a moderating force in the conflict.

**Trends and Outlook**

The majority view among independent analysts is that the US-Iran conflict is accelerating a broader shift in global power dynamics. The US military’s logistical and technological strains are creating openings for rivals, particularly China, while the war’s political fallout is destabilizing both allied and adversary states. The humanitarian crisis in Iran continues to worsen, and the risk of regional spillover remains high.

Looking ahead, unless there is a dramatic diplomatic breakthrough, the conflict is expected to drag on, with rising costs for all parties and growing unpredictability in both regional and global security environments. The erosion of US and European credibility, coupled with the empowerment of alternative power blocs, signals a potentially lasting transformation of the international order.
