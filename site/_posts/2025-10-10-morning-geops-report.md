As global tensions persist, the past 12 hours have seen new developments on the Israel-Gaza ceasefire, shifting narratives around the humanitarian crisis, the Russia-Ukraine conflict, and the growing intersection of technology and geopolitics.

**Gaza Ceasefire: Fragility and Continued Violence**

Despite high-profile announcements of a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas, fighting on the ground has not ceased. **Caitlin Johnstone** observes that “Israel continued its military operations in Gaza,” even after the agreement, though she notes the deal may still bring a temporary reduction in violence and some easing of humanitarian conditions. **Larry C. Johnson** points out the déjà vu of such ceasefire announcements, recalling previous deals that quickly unraveled amid complex prisoner exchanges and ongoing hostilities. The majority of analysts remain skeptical, viewing the ceasefire as a tactical pause rather than a genuine step toward lasting peace.

**Narrative Control and Public Grief**

Amid the ongoing humanitarian crisis in Gaza, efforts to shape public perception have intensified. **Jonathan Cook** highlights how “politicians, police, and media are attempting to isolate and shame those grieving the deaths of children in Gaza,” urging the public to resist such narratives and recognize their shared humanity. This campaign to control the narrative, according to Cook, risks deepening polarization and suppressing legitimate expressions of grief and solidarity.

**Russia-Ukraine War: Escalating Infrastructure Strikes**

On the Eastern front, the Russia-Ukraine conflict continues its destructive cycle. **Gilbert Doctorow** emphasizes the reciprocal escalation of strikes on energy infrastructure, noting the lack of clarity in Western media about which side initiates these attacks. Doctorow also points out that Ukraine’s counterstrikes on Russian refineries, often using American-supplied weapons, remain underreported. The consensus is that the conflict is locked in a costly stalemate, with both sides suffering and little progress toward resolution.

**Geopolitics of Technology: Surveillance and Influence**

The intersection of technology, geopolitics, and military interests is drawing renewed attention. **Miguel Ruíz** discusses the strategic implications of platforms like TikTok and Oracle in the context of Israeli military and intelligence operations, suggesting that control over digital infrastructure is becoming a key front in global power struggles. This aligns with broader concerns about surveillance, information warfare, and the manipulation of public discourse.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trends point to continued volatility: ceasefire announcements in Gaza are met with skepticism as violence persists, while efforts to control public sentiment around the humanitarian crisis risk further polarization. In Ukraine, the war’s destructive stalemate endures, with infrastructure attacks escalating and little hope for peace. Meanwhile, the role of technology in surveillance and influence operations is set to become even more central to geopolitical competition. Analysts predict ongoing instability, with the gap between official narratives and public sentiment likely to widen in the weeks ahead.
