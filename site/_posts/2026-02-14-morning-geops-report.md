As mid-February 2026 advances, the geopolitical environment remains tense and fluid, with new developments highlighting the deepening rifts between major powers, the fragility of Western institutions, and the growing risks of economic and social instability.

**Russia-China-Iran Axis: Strategic Pact and Expanded Support**

A key shift in the balance of power is the strengthening of the Russia-China-Iran partnership. **Larry C. Johnson** reports that Moscow and Beijing are “significantly enhancing their support for Iran through a Trilateral Strategic Pact and various forms of assistance,” directly countering U.S. efforts to isolate Tehran and Havana in the aftermath of the 2025 Iran-Israel war. This expanded cooperation includes military, economic, and diplomatic backing, and is seen as a direct response to Washington’s attempts to enforce sanctions and project power in the Middle East and the Caribbean. Analysts widely view this alignment as a major obstacle to U.S. strategy, complicating any efforts to pressure Iran or Cuba into concessions.

**Europe’s Decline and Internal Strains**

Europe’s waning influence is becoming increasingly apparent. **Richard D. Wolff** describes the continent’s trajectory as a “historical decline from a central role in global civilization to a secondary status in the modern economy,” now exacerbated by military and economic crises. With political elites seeking to address these challenges by shifting burdens onto the working class, the risk of class conflict and social unrest is rising. This internal fragility is mirrored in the energy crisis facing Ukraine and the EU, as highlighted by **Larry C. Johnson**, who notes widespread shortages and infrastructure damage that have left millions without reliable heating or electricity.

**US Domestic Turmoil: Legal and Political Escalation**

On the U.S. domestic front, the Trump administration’s aggressive posture is extending beyond foreign policy into the legal and educational spheres. **Chris Hedges** details the administration’s “escalating war on Harvard,” where a civil rights investigation into alleged discrimination against white applicants has morphed into a broader political attack on higher education, with threats of billion-dollar penalties and criminal prosecution. This campaign is emblematic of a wider trend of politicization and retribution, further polarizing American society and undermining institutional stability.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is toward greater polarization and multipolarity. The Russia-China-Iran axis is consolidating as a formidable counterweight to U.S.-led alliances, raising the stakes in ongoing diplomatic and military standoffs. Europe faces a period of introspection and potential upheaval as it grapples with diminished influence and internal crises. In the U.S., the fusion of political, legal, and cultural battles is deepening domestic divides.

Looking ahead, analysts expect continued brinkmanship and proxy competition, with few signs of imminent de-escalation. The risk of miscalculation remains high, particularly as economic and institutional pressures mount across the West. The coming weeks are likely to see further tests of alliance cohesion, domestic resilience, and the capacity of global leaders to manage overlapping crises.
