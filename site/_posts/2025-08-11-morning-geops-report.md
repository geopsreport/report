As the Middle East and Eurasia remain at the center of global attention, the past 12 hours have brought new developments in media ownership, U.S. military policy in Latin America, and the evolving diplomatic landscape in the South Caucasus.

**Media Consolidation and Political Influence in the U.S.**

The American media environment is undergoing a significant transformation following the acquisition of Paramount Global by David Ellison, son of billionaire Larry Ellison. **Chris Hedges** reports that Ellison’s takeover will bring CBS News and other major outlets under the control of Israel’s largest U.S. donor, with plans to steer coverage toward a more right-wing, pro-Trump, and pro-Israel stance. Notably, pro-Israel journalist Bari Weiss is being considered for a leading role in shaping the network’s political direction. Analysts warn this consolidation could further erode independent journalism and reinforce partisan narratives, intensifying concerns about press freedom and public trust.

**U.S. Expands Military Operations in Latin America**

In a marked escalation of U.S. foreign policy, President Trump has authorized direct military action against Latin American drug cartels. **Chris Hedges** details that Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro has been labeled a cartel leader, with a bounty placed on his capture. This move signals a willingness to deploy U.S. forces on foreign soil, raising the stakes in U.S.-Latin American relations and potentially destabilizing the region. The majority of analysts view this as part of a broader trend toward unilateral U.S. interventions, with uncertain long-term consequences for regional security and diplomatic ties.

**South Caucasus: The “Trump Bridge” Corridor and Regional Pushback**

Diplomatic maneuvering in the South Caucasus continues to reshape regional dynamics. The recent signing of the “Trump Bridge” corridor agreement—brokered by President Trump and signed at the White House by Azerbaijan’s President Ilham Aliyev and Armenia’s Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan—has provoked strong opposition from Iran and Russia. **Elijah J. Magnier** explains that the corridor, linking Azerbaijan to its Nakhchivan exclave via Armenian territory, is perceived by Moscow and Tehran as a direct challenge to their influence and a strategic shift in transit routes. This development is expected to heighten geopolitical competition in the region and could trigger further diplomatic and economic countermeasures from Russia and Iran.

**Trends and Outlook**

The convergence of increased media consolidation, assertive U.S. military actions abroad, and aggressive diplomatic initiatives underscores a period of intensifying polarization and strategic competition. Analysts broadly predict that the U.S. will continue to leverage both media and military power to shape global narratives and outcomes, while regional actors such as Russia and Iran will seek to push back against perceived encroachments. The coming days are likely to see further shifts in media coverage, heightened tensions in Latin America, and growing friction in the South Caucasus, all contributing to an increasingly fragmented and unstable international order.
