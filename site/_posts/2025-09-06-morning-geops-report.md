As the global order faces renewed turbulence, the past 12 hours have brought fresh developments in India’s foreign policy alignment, the legal aftermath of 9/11, and the intensifying debate over US militarization and the Gaza crisis.

**India’s Diplomatic Pivot: From Eurasia to Europe**

Amid shifting alliances, India is recalibrating its international posture. According to **M. K. Bhadrakumar**, Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s 50-minute meeting with President Putin in Tianjin was overshadowed by India’s discomfort at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) event. Bhadrakumar notes that India is now “distancing itself from the so-called ‘Tianjin spirit’” and turning toward the European Union, signaling a strategic pivot away from the Eurasian bloc. This move is interpreted by analysts as a response to both regional frictions and the perceived limitations of the SCO framework, with India seeking to balance relations amid intensifying US-China and Russia-West rivalries.

**9/11 Lawsuit Against Saudi Arabia Moves Forward**

In a significant legal development, the long-running lawsuit against Saudi Arabia regarding the 9/11 attacks has been cleared to proceed to trial. **Ray McGovern** highlights that plaintiffs have presented new information challenging the FBI’s earlier assessment that two US-based Saudi officials unknowingly assisted the hijackers. This breakthrough could have far-reaching diplomatic implications, potentially straining US-Saudi relations and reigniting debates over state accountability for terrorism.

**US Militarization and Gaza: Escalating Critique**

The US government’s increasingly militarized stance continues to draw scrutiny. **Larry C. Johnson** criticizes President Trump’s decision to rename the Department of Defense as the “Department of War,” arguing that it “undermines the notion of defense and contradicts any aspirations for a Nobel Peace Prize.” Meanwhile, **Caitlin Johnstone** underscores the moral imperative to oppose what she calls the ongoing genocide in Gaza, insisting that “opposing an ongoing genocide requires no justification or explanation.” These perspectives reflect a growing divide between official US policy and both domestic and international public opinion, particularly as the humanitarian crisis in Gaza deepens and Washington’s support for Israel remains steadfast.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to further fragmentation of traditional alliances, with India’s pivot emblematic of broader realignments in the Global South. Legal and moral challenges to US and allied policies are intensifying, from the courtroom to the court of public opinion. Analysts expect continued diplomatic turbulence, with the upcoming UN General Assembly likely to serve as a flashpoint for debates over Palestinian statehood, accountability for state-sponsored violence, and the legitimacy of military interventions. The prospects for de-escalation remain slim, as polarization and contestation over the global order deepen.
