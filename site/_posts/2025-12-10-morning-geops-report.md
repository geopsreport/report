As the geopolitical landscape continues to fragment, the last 12 hours have brought new revelations on covert operations in the Ukraine conflict, shifting dynamics in Asian defense procurement, and the persistent failures of Russia’s strategic missile program. Analysts are highlighting the limits of Western intelligence operations, growing competition in the Asian arms market, and the challenges facing Russia’s military modernization.

**Ukraine: Operation Spiderweb and Western Intelligence Limits**

Fresh details have emerged about Ukraine’s Operation Spiderweb, a covert 18-month campaign involving significant Western intelligence support. According to **Larry C. Johnson**, the operation culminated in a high-profile June 2025 attack on Russia, but “ultimately failed to hinder Russia’s military efforts despite being portrayed as a success.” Johnson notes that while Western media and officials have lauded the operation, Russian advances on the battlefield have continued largely unabated, underscoring the limited impact of clandestine initiatives on the broader conflict. The majority of analysts agree that such covert actions, though disruptive, have not shifted the strategic balance, and skepticism is growing about their effectiveness as a substitute for diplomatic engagement.

**Asia: Eurofighter Typhoon Scores Win Over China in Bangladesh**

In a notable development in the Asian arms race, Bangladesh has signed a Letter of Intent with Italy’s Leonardo for the potential purchase of 10–16 Tranche 4 Eurofighter Typhoons. **Daniel L. Davis** reports this as “a significant win for the Eurofighter program over Chinese competition,” potentially making Bangladesh the first Asian customer for the advanced European fighter. This move signals a diversification of defense partnerships in South Asia and is seen as a setback for China’s ambitions to expand its military exports in the region. Analysts highlight that this deal reflects broader trends of Asian states seeking alternatives to Chinese and Russian hardware amid concerns over reliability and political alignment.

**Russia: Sarmat ICBM’s Continued Failures**

Russia’s efforts to modernize its strategic arsenal have suffered another blow, as the RS-28 Sarmat intercontinental ballistic missile—touted by President Putin as “unstoppable”—has failed yet another test. **Daniel L. Davis** points out that this latest setback “continues a pattern of missed operational timelines since its unveiling in 2018.” The repeated failures cast doubt on Russia’s claims of technological superiority and raise questions about the credibility of its nuclear deterrent. The prevailing opinion among defense analysts is that these issues could undermine Moscow’s strategic posture, especially as it faces mounting pressure on multiple fronts.

**Trends and Outlook**

The latest developments reinforce a pattern of skepticism toward high-profile covert operations and military modernization claims. In Ukraine, the limits of clandestine warfare are becoming apparent, with little effect on the overall trajectory of the conflict. In Asia, the Eurofighter’s success in Bangladesh suggests a more multipolar arms market and growing competition with China. Meanwhile, Russia’s persistent missile failures highlight the challenges of sustaining a credible deterrent in the face of technological and operational hurdles. Analysts expect these trends to continue, with incremental changes and persistent volatility likely to define the coming weeks.
