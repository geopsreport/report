As the US-Israeli war on Iran enters its eleventh day, the conflict’s regional and global reverberations continue to intensify, with new developments underscoring the deepening economic, military, and political crises.

**Strait of Hormuz: Economic Shock Looms**

A major new threat has emerged as Iran appears poised to mine the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies. **Jack Buckby** reports that US intelligence has observed Iranian naval preparations consistent with large-scale mining operations. **Larry C. Johnson** warns that a sustained closure of the Strait would “severely disrupt global oil, LNG, and fertilizer exports,” potentially triggering “unprecedented economic shocks” affecting energy markets, food security, and the fiscal stability of numerous countries. Oil prices have already surged above $120 per barrel, with markets reacting with volatility and anxiety.

**Military Stalemate and Coalition Strain**

On the battlefield, the initial US-Israeli “shock and awe” campaign has failed to achieve a decisive breakthrough. **Elijah J. Magnier** notes that President Trump’s expectation of intimidating Iran into submission has backfired, with Iranian forces continuing to retaliate and the conflict settling into a protracted stalemate. The Pentagon is now shifting to lower-cost JDAM munitions as stocks of Tomahawk cruise missiles dwindle, a move described by **Daniel L. Davis** as necessary to “conserve high-end munitions for future operations in the Indo-Pacific.” This highlights growing concerns about the coalition’s ability to sustain high-intensity operations, especially if a second front emerges elsewhere.

**International Fallout and Political Doubts**

The war’s diplomatic and political repercussions are mounting. **Peter Cronau** details Australia’s deepening involvement, with the deployment of a spy plane to the UAE and expanded hosting of US bases, signaling a stronger commitment by Washington’s allies even as domestic opposition grows. Meanwhile, **Chris Hedges** critiques the “imperial arrogance” of the US and Israel, noting that the display of military power is increasingly seen as overreach, especially as civilian casualties mount and international condemnation intensifies.

**Trends and Outlook**

The majority of independent analysts agree that the war has reached a dangerous impasse. The risk of further escalation—particularly involving the Strait of Hormuz and Lebanon—remains high, with the potential for global economic shocks and worsening humanitarian crises. Calls for a diplomatic off-ramp are growing louder, but with both sides entrenched and civilian suffering mounting, a rapid de-escalation appears unlikely in the near term. The prevailing trend is one of strategic stalemate, operational strain, and increasing international isolation for the US-led coalition.
