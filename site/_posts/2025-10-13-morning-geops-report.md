As the Gaza ceasefire remains tenuous and the Ukraine conflict persists, the past 12 hours have brought new revelations of political maneuvering, foreign interference, and the fragility of diplomatic efforts across multiple fronts. The gap between official narratives and the realities on the ground continues to widen, with skepticism mounting over the intentions of key actors.

**Israel Signals Imminent End to Gaza Ceasefire**

Despite the ongoing ceasefire, Israeli officials are openly preparing to resume military operations in Gaza. **Caitlin Johnstone** reports that “Israel's top officials plan to end the Gaza ceasefire after recovering hostages, focusing on destroying Hamas's terror tunnels,” with no agreement from Hamas on demilitarization. This public stance reinforces widespread doubts about the ceasefire’s durability and suggests that Israeli leadership views the pause as a tactical maneuver rather than a step toward lasting peace. The lack of substantive progress on core issues continues to undermine international confidence in the process.

**Allegations of Corruption and Influence in Ukraine Policy**

Fresh scrutiny has emerged over Western involvement in Ukraine, with new allegations implicating former UK Prime Minister Boris Johnson. **Larry C. Johnson** details that Johnson received a $1 million donation from political donor Christopher Harborne—who has significant interests in the Ukraine war—shortly after leaving office, and was accompanied by Harborne during a recent visit to Ukraine. This revelation has fueled concerns about personal enrichment and the role of private interests in prolonging the conflict, echoing broader anxieties about the integrity of Western policy toward Ukraine.

**Social Media and National Security: Growing Concerns Over Influence Operations**

The intersection of national security and information control is drawing increased attention. **Chris Hedges** highlights that numerous former national security agents have been employed at major social media companies, with many deleting their profiles after being exposed by MintPress News. This development raises questions about the extent of intelligence community influence over online discourse, particularly in politically sensitive contexts such as the Israel-Palestine conflict and the Ukraine war.

**Trends and Outlook**

The latest developments underscore a pattern of deepening mistrust—between governments and their publics, and among international actors. The majority of analysts agree that the Gaza ceasefire is unlikely to hold, given Israel’s stated intentions and the absence of meaningful concessions. Allegations of corruption and influence-peddling in Ukraine further erode confidence in Western leadership. Meanwhile, the entanglement of social media with national security agencies points to a future where information warfare and narrative control will play an even greater role in shaping public perception and policy.

Looking ahead, the risk of renewed violence in Gaza is high, and the Ukraine conflict shows little sign of resolution amid mounting scandals. The credibility of Western institutions continues to decline, with most observers expecting further polarization, diplomatic gridlock, and an intensification of information battles in the coming days.
