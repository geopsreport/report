As the US-Israel war on Iran enters its third week, the conflict’s operational and political dynamics continue to evolve, with new developments underscoring the growing risks for all parties and the intensifying global ramifications.

**Narrative Legitimacy and the Nuclear Question**

A significant new angle in the discourse is the challenge to the war’s underlying justification. **Vijay Prashad**, cited by **Ray McGovern**, asserts that “there are no nuclear weapons in Iran,” likening the current rationale for war to the discredited claims of weapons of mass destruction that preceded the 2003 Iraq invasion. This perspective is gaining traction among independent analysts, fueling skepticism about the legitimacy of the campaign and increasing international criticism, particularly as civilian casualties mount.

**US Air Power: Stealth Assets and Operational Strain**

On the military front, the US continues to rely heavily on its advanced stealth platforms. **Daniel L. Davis** highlights the operational evolution of the B-2 Spirit stealth bomber, noting its transition from a deterrent to a “key offensive asset” in Operation Epic Fury. The historic “elephant walk” of 12 B-2s at Whiteman Air Force Base in 2024 is emblematic of this shift. However, Davis also points to the high operational costs and the strain on these assets, especially as the US faces a critical shortage of Tomahawk missiles and interceptor stocks, raising questions about the sustainability of such high-intensity operations if the conflict expands or if new crises emerge elsewhere.

**Credibility of Missile Defense Systems**

Meanwhile, the credibility of advanced air defense systems is under scrutiny. **Daniel L. Davis** reports that Russia’s S-500 Prometey, touted as a counter to US stealth fighters, has shown “underwhelming real-world performance” in Crimea, casting doubt on the effectiveness of such systems in modern high-intensity warfare. This has implications for both the defense of US and allied assets and the broader arms race dynamic, as adversaries reassess the value of their own air defense investments.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing consensus among analysts is that the war’s legitimacy crisis is deepening, with the nuclear justification increasingly questioned and the humanitarian toll rising. The US military’s reliance on high-end stealth assets is both a strength and a vulnerability, given resource constraints and the risk of strategic overstretch. The disappointing performance of next-generation air defense systems further complicates the security calculus for all sides.

Looking ahead, absent a diplomatic breakthrough, the conflict is expected to remain protracted and unpredictable, with the risk of regional spillover and global instability growing. The erosion of public trust in official narratives and the mounting operational challenges suggest that the war could have lasting consequences for US influence and the international order.
