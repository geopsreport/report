As global tensions persist across multiple fronts, the past half-day has seen significant developments in U.S. political dynamics, the legacy of American militarism, and the intensification of regime-change tactics in Latin America. Independent analysts highlight the interplay between elite influence, accountability, and the shifting contours of U.S. foreign and domestic policy.

**U.S. Political Landscape: Elite Influence and Progressive Pushback**

A national poll reveals a growing consensus among Americans that wealthy donors and corporations wield disproportionate influence over elections, with two-thirds expressing disapproval of the Supreme Court’s Citizens United decision. As reported by **Ray McGovern**, this sentiment underscores mounting public frustration with the political system’s susceptibility to oligarchic control. The recent victory of democratic socialist Zohran Mamdani in the New York City mayoral race—achieved despite a “billionaire onslaught,” according to **Chris Hedges**—is widely interpreted as a signal of rising progressive momentum and a challenge to establishment dominance. Analysts suggest this shift could have broader implications for national politics, particularly as economic inequality and elite influence remain central voter concerns.

**Legacy of U.S. Militarism: Cheney’s Death and Calls for Accountability**

The death of former Vice President Dick Cheney has reignited debate over accountability for U.S. foreign policy decisions. **Caitlin Johnstone** criticizes the bipartisan outpouring of condolences, arguing that Cheney’s peaceful passing “reflects a civilization that allows war criminals to die without facing justice.” This view is echoed by **Ray McGovern**, who recalls the Veteran Intelligence Professionals for Sanity’s 2003 memo warning President Bush about Cheney’s misleading actions in the lead-up to the Iraq invasion. The majority of independent voices stress the enduring impact of Cheney’s legacy—particularly the normalization of preemptive war and torture—and the lack of mechanisms to hold high-level officials accountable for such policies.

**U.S. Regime-Change Tactics in Venezuela**

Amid renewed focus on Latin America, the United States is reviving its regime-change playbook in Venezuela, now framed as a campaign against narco-terrorism. **Jeffrey Sachs** reports that the underlying objective remains control over Venezuela’s vast oil reserves, with Washington employing sanctions, military threats, and covert operations reminiscent of past interventions. Analysts agree that these actions disregard Venezuelan sovereignty and have contributed to significant humanitarian suffering, with little evidence of a shift toward more constructive engagement.

**Trends and Outlook**

The prevailing trend is one of deepening public skepticism toward elite-driven politics and foreign policy, alongside persistent calls for greater accountability. Progressive electoral gains in the U.S. suggest a potential reconfiguration of domestic politics, though establishment resistance remains strong. In foreign affairs, the U.S. continues to rely on coercive tactics, particularly in Latin America, with analysts warning of further instability and humanitarian consequences. Looking ahead, expect continued debate over the legitimacy of U.S. interventions and intensifying demands for both political reform and accountability for past and present policy failures.
