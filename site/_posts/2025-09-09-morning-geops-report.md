As the international system faces mounting instability, the past 12 hours have brought new revelations in Middle East diplomacy, covert operations targeting Iran, and the ongoing contest over sanctions and economic warfare.

**US Envoys and Netanyahu: Contrasting Diplomatic Approaches**

In the context of deepening crisis in Gaza and persistent US support for Israel, the role of American envoys has come under renewed scrutiny. **Elijah J. Magnier** details the divergent strategies of US envoys Steve Witkoff and Thomas Barrack in dealing with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. While Witkoff has successfully navigated Netanyahu’s survivalist political tactics, Barrack’s attempts at good faith diplomacy have floundered. Magnier notes that “Netanyahu’s illusory promises and shifting priorities” have left even close US allies frustrated, highlighting the challenges facing any diplomatic initiative that does not account for Israel’s domestic political calculus.

**Israeli Intelligence Campaign Targets Iran with US Social Media Influencer**

A new report by **Max Blumenthal** exposes an Israeli intelligence-linked operation that sought to recruit Iranian scientists and foment regime change via online campaigns. Notably, Atlanta-based comedian Desi Banks, with a large US social media following, was involved in efforts to reach Iranians and encourage defection. This revelation underscores the increasingly hybrid and unconventional nature of intelligence operations in the region, as well as the willingness of state actors to leverage Western influencers in pursuit of strategic objectives.

**US Sanctions Strategy Faces Skepticism Amid Russian Resilience**

On the economic front, the US Treasury’s latest push to collapse the Russian economy through new sanctions on oil and gas exports to China and India is being met with skepticism. **Larry C. Johnson** argues that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent “misunderstands the resilience of Russia’s economy and its strategic partnerships,” pointing out that previous sanctions have failed to achieve their intended effect. The majority of analysts concur that such measures are unlikely to significantly weaken Russia, especially as alternative trade and financial mechanisms proliferate within the SCO and BRICS frameworks.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to the limits of traditional Western diplomatic and economic tools in the face of adaptive adversaries and shifting alliances. The exposure of covert influence operations and the ineffectiveness of sanctions highlight the need for new approaches as the global order becomes more contested. Analysts expect continued diplomatic friction over Gaza, further revelations about intelligence activities, and persistent challenges to Western economic leverage. The coming weeks are likely to see intensified competition in both overt and covert arenas, with little prospect for de-escalation.
