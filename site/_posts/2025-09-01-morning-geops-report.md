As September begins, the geopolitical landscape is marked by deepening realignments and intensifying public scrutiny of official narratives, with new developments in Eurasian alliances, the Israel-Gaza conflict, and the shifting global order.

**Eurasian Bloc Consolidates Amid U.S. Policy Missteps**

The ongoing Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) conference has underscored the growing cohesion among key Eurasian powers. **Larry C. Johnson** highlights that “China, India, and Russia are solidifying their ties rather than drifting apart,” challenging Western assumptions that these states can be easily divided. Johnson points to U.S. tariffs on India as a catalyst pushing New Delhi closer to BRICS and away from Washington’s influence. Indian Prime Minister Modi’s leadership role in BRICS, alongside improving India-China relations, signals a decisive shift in global power dynamics and further erodes U.S. hegemony in the region. The majority view is that the West’s attempts to manipulate alliances are increasingly counterproductive, accelerating the emergence of a multipolar world order.

**Türkiye’s Economic Break with Israel: Public Pressure and Regional Impact**

Türkiye’s comprehensive economic severance from Israel continues to reverberate across the region. **Juan Cole** reports that President Erdogan’s administration, under intense public pressure and amid widespread protests, has enacted a full ban on Israeli ships and overflights, aiming to enforce a previously porous boycott. The Turkish parliament’s declaration of Gaza as a site of genocide has further galvanized public opinion, compelling the government to adopt more definitive measures. Analysts note that while some trade—such as Azerbaijani oil shipments—may still bypass these restrictions, the move marks a significant escalation and could prompt similar actions from other regional actors.

**Moral Reckoning in Israel: Internal Critique of Gaza Policy**

Within Israel, a growing chorus of internal criticism is emerging over the stark disparity in valuing Israeli and Palestinian lives. **Caitlin Johnstone**, citing Israeli journalist Gideon Levy, notes the “deep frustration with the stark disparity in how Israelis value the lives of their own citizens compared to Palestinians.” Levy’s critique of the Israeli protest movement’s focus on hostages, while largely ignoring Palestinian suffering, reflects a broader moral reckoning. He attributes this to a pervasive racism that dehumanizes Palestinians, calling it a fundamental issue within Israeli society. This internal debate is contributing to mounting international scrutiny and calls for accountability regarding Israeli actions in Gaza.

**Trends and Outlook**

The dominant trends point to a rapid consolidation of Eurasian alliances, diminishing Western leverage, and a growing willingness among regional actors to challenge U.S. and Israeli policies. Public opinion—both in Türkiye and within Israel—is playing an increasingly decisive role in shaping government actions and narratives. Analysts widely predict that these dynamics will further entrench multipolarity and intensify calls for accountability in conflict zones. The overall outlook is one of accelerating polarization, with diplomatic breakthroughs remaining elusive and the risk of broader regional realignments on the rise.
