As the global landscape remains volatile, the past 12 hours have brought renewed scrutiny to European diplomacy on Iran, the deepening crisis in Gaza, and the shifting dynamics of Eurasian alliances, with independent analysts highlighting both immediate risks and longer-term trends.

**Europe’s Iran ‘Snapback’: Symbolic Diplomacy or Escalation?**

France, Germany, and the UK have officially initiated the UN “snapback” mechanism to restore sanctions on Iran, citing breaches of the 2015 nuclear agreement. According to **Elijah J. Magnier**, this move launches a 30-day process intended to “save nuclear diplomacy and avoid another war,” but it also risks escalation in the wake of recent bombings targeting Iran. Tehran has responded by questioning Europe’s true intentions and threatening to end cooperation with the IAEA, while Russia and China have voiced strong opposition at the UN. Analysts widely agree that Europe’s action underscores its declining autonomy, as it aligns with US and Israeli interests, and may ultimately increase the risk of conflict rather than foster peace.

**Gaza: UN Infrastructure Under Assault, Humanitarian Crisis Intensifies**

The situation in Gaza continues to deteriorate, with new attention on Israel’s systematic targeting of UNRWA infrastructure. **Mara Kronenfeld**, executive director of UNRWA USA, emphasizes that the destruction of UN facilities represents a “broader attack on civilian life for millions of Palestinians.” This comes as famine conditions persist and international condemnation grows, yet concrete accountability measures remain absent. Meanwhile, **Richard Falk** critiques the ambivalence of Western liberal media, arguing that coverage often highlights humanitarian suffering without demanding accountability or challenging Israel’s territorial ambitions.

**Eurasian Realignment: India, China, and Russia at the SCO**

The Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in Tianjin continues to spotlight the strengthening ties between India, China, and Russia. **Gilbert Doctorow** notes that while India and China’s combined economic power still lags behind the US, the “warm interactions between Modi and Putin” suggest a deepening partnership amid global tensions. The summit is seen as consolidating Eurasian and Global South interests, with the SCO serving as a platform for these states to assert greater independence from Western-led institutions.

**Trends and Outlook**

Across sources, there is consensus that Europe’s diplomatic maneuvers on Iran reflect a symbolic effort constrained by US policy, with the risk of escalation outweighing prospects for renewed diplomacy. In Gaza, the destruction of UN infrastructure and persistent famine highlight a worsening humanitarian disaster, with international outrage but little effective action. The Eurasian bloc, meanwhile, is steadily consolidating, though economic challenges remain. Analysts predict continued polarization, with Europe’s declining autonomy, deepening humanitarian crises, and the rise of multipolar alliances shaping the global order in the months ahead.
